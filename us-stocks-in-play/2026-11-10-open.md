## The headline

**The hot CPI print owns the tape, and Nvidia is the trade that runs against it.** October CPI came in at **+0.4% m/m against a +0.3% consensus, with core at +0.3% against +0.2%**, and the reaction was immediate and cross-asset: the **10-year jumped to 4.987% (+12.3bp)**, the dollar firmed (**DXY +0.45%**), **gold fell to $4,056 (-1.41%)**, **silver -2.84%**, and every long-duration growth name was repriced. And **the AI-power complex broke**: **GE Vernova -7.13% on $1.55bn** is the single biggest dislocation on the board, after **GLJ Research started coverage at Sell with a $470 target** and the data-centre backlash turned political overnight — voters rejected data centres in **40 of 41** tracked ballot measures, and newly elected officials in Texas, Georgia and Ohio have pledged to pause approvals for large AI data-centre projects.

The caveat that changes how this should be read: **this is a rates repricing, not an AI-demand repricing, and the chips are still bid.** At 10:34 ET the broad tape is flat-to-lower (**Nasdaq Composite -0.32%, S&P 500 -0.33%, Dow -0.40%, Russell 2000 -0.62%**) and the **VIX's +5.5% spike at 09:43 has already faded back to 20.10** — that is a rate move, not a risk-off. Meanwhile **SOXX +0.84%** and **SMH +0.17%**, and the board's own largest name is a single-stock event, not a macro one: **Nvidia -4.10% on $19.8bn** of turnover in twenty minutes (pace 5.33) as the board's investigation into CEO Jensen Huang persists and the odds of him leaving the chair were quoted at **18%** — while **AMD +3.23% on $5.4bn** and the rest of the complex is carried by **Anthropic's IPO** (Google, Nvidia and Microsoft putting $26bn in).

Two things a trader should know before the close. **PepGen +145.61%** on positive Phase 2 FREEDOM2-DM1 data is the largest mover on the board by an order of magnitude, and it is real money — **$357.6m** of turnover in twenty minutes. ## Method and caveats

- **Every price is the 09:50 ET state, rebuilt from 5-minute bars.** The universe was pulled between 09:52 and 09:56 ET and the bars between 10:09 and 10:21 ET. Bar timestamps are the START of each 5-minute interval, so the **price at 09:50 is the close of the last bar stamped at or before 09:45** and the **session volume to 09:50 is the sum of every bar at or before 09:50**. The market-context quotes are the bridge's own 10:19–10:34 ET prints, so they are **later than the 09:50 board** and read as the tape *after* the cut.
- **The prior close is reconstructed from the same 5-minute series** — the last non-null close of the 2026-11-09 session — because the `chartPreviousClose` of a multi-day range is the close *before* the range starts, not the prior session. Using it turns every move into a ten-day move. The reconstruction was verified against Yahoo's own daily bar on 80 names (4 of 80 off by more than 1%).
- **The gap verdicts cover 20 minutes, not the session.** `held` = 'held for 20 minutes'. The pre-close fire at 15:50 ET owns the held-into-the-bell question, and its gap annex is a different screen with a different floor.
- **The pace measure is a screening measure, not a measurement.** The 0.125 opening-share constant is regime-dependent — the measured daily median spans **10.77% and 16.49%** — so a published pace of 2.0 is roughly 1.8–2.3 on a different session's regime. The 5-minute series is reconciled to the daily-bar basis by a **per-symbol factor measured on the 2026-11-09 session** (median **0.8918**); the residual risk is that the missing ~11% of volume is not perfectly proportional through the day, so a single name's pace can carry **±20%** of error. **The pace is not comparable with the pre-close slot's RVol** — the column is labelled `pace` everywhere, including in the CSV.
- **Screen completeness:** the universe was pulled in three unpaginated calls, each verified against its own `totalCount`; a paginated pull of the same universe silently returns duplicate rows and was not used. The 09:50 trigger was evaluated on the **pre-filtered subset** of 7,889 symbols (the documented union of turnover ≥ $100k, |move| ≥ 2pp and no-price legs), and **839 of the 7,857 pulled symbols printed no 5-minute bar at all before 09:50** — those are names that did not trade in the first twenty minutes, and they are absent from the 09:50 reconstruction. Every one of the **531** published names is priced and tiered — **no top-N cap, no unpriced tail**. The trigger screen is on the **09:50 reconstruction**, not on the scanner's live quote, so a name that was more than 3% at 09:50 and flat by 10:00 ET is **not** a hole in this board.
- **Not verified:** the proximate cause of several large moves could not be confirmed with a dated source and they are published with the price, the pace and **no catalyst claim** — among them **EQPT -22.28%**, **FF -41.19%**, **MNTK -38.95%**, **EMAT -35.91%** and **TLSI -27.02%**, which printed on thin turnover before any headline could be dated. **No low-float flags are published anywhere on this board** because the scanner's shares-outstanding column is null for every row — the low-float rule could not be evaluated, not because every name is liquid.
- **This is a live board, not a settled one.** Nothing here is a closing price, and the pre-close fire at 15:50 ET publishes the settled version of the same screen with `RVol` and the full-session volume floor.

**degraded_sources: 4** — (1) the TradingView scanner's `halted` column is null on every row, so **the halt trigger could not be evaluated** this session; (2) the scanner's shares-outstanding column is null on every row, so **the low-float rule could not be applied** and no low-float flags are published; (3) **32 of the 7,889 Yahoo 5-minute requests returned HTTP 404** (dead or unlisted symbols), which are absent from the 09:50 reconstruction; (4) the **cookie'd** Yahoo chart session was throttled by the egress edge mid-run, so the pull was re-run cookie-less and **every published number comes from the completed cookie-less pass**.
