## The headline

**The blue wave split the AI trade in half, and the Dow told you which half the market wanted.** Democrats took the House and the Senate on Tuesday night's midterms, and the first session with the result priced was a violent, textbook rotation: the **Dow +3.03% (+1,570 pts)**, the **S&P 500 +1.58%**, the **Nasdaq Composite +1.44%**, the **Nasdaq-100 only +0.91%** — the index-level gap between the Dow and the NDX is the whole story, because the Dow's gain was carried by companies that *spend* on AI and by the consumer, financials and healthcare that a Democratic Congress is expected to treat better, while the Nasdaq's gain was held back by the companies that *sell* the build-out.

**The trade: money out of the AI hardware, money into the AI spenders.** **MSFT +6.85% to a record above $558, AMZN +6.74%, AAPL +6.03% to an all-time high $354.33, GOOGL +3.84%** — the "slower build repairs free cash flow" thesis, with Texas governor Gina Hinojosa's promised **data-centre moratorium** and similar pledges in Ohio and Georgia as the mechanism. Against that: **MU -7.30% ($23.2bn — the largest dollar volume on the board), SNDK -8.57%, MRVL -8.20%, LITE -9.44%, INTC -4.87%, DELL -9.74%, STX -7.93%**, and the neocloud/power complex **CRWV -10.42%, NBIS -7.59%, IREN -11.43%, VRT -13.45%, GEV -9.29%, VST -13.70%**, **CIFR -21.24%, CLSK -16.99%, HUT -23.81%**. Dan Ives' shorthand was that "10% of datacenters will not happen now"; Barron's had the cleanest headline of the day: *"AI Data Center Backlash Is Real. The Blue Wave Takes Out Energy Stocks."*

The caveat that changes how this should be read: **this was a political repricing, not an earnings verdict, and it landed on top of a tape that was already mispricing the same stocks.** Relative volume is *below* 1.0 on most of the losers — **MU 1.15, SNDK 1.08, CRWV 0.91, NBIS 0.95, STX 0.94** — so the selling was not distribution; it was a **short-covering-free, thesis-driven de-rate**, and the names that actually had company news today (ANET, CVS, DDOG, FIS, UNM, MOD, DOX, NRIX, KD, CRCT, HELE, CMTG, TREE, DUOL) moved on their own prints in **both** directions. The second caveat is that the **macro tape was violently benign**: the **VIX -19.59% to 16.75**, the **10-year -5.8bp to 4.894%**, and **crude -7.69% to $88.70** on the US-Iran memorandum that the previous digest's carry flagged. A vol crush plus a lower discount rate is the perfect backdrop for a rotation to run cleanly — and it is also why the losers were marked down rather than bid.

Three genuinely new pieces of information, rather than continuations of Monday's board:

1. **The hyperscaler trade is now explicitly the opposite of the hardware trade.** "Dem sweep is bullish AI because everyone spends less on capex and diffusion has time to catch up to the buildout" (BuccoCapital) is the bull case that took MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL and META higher and took MU, SNDK, DELL, CRDO and the neoclouds lower. Cramer's version is the cleanest: **"Apple winner — slowdown in build means inventories can build of DRAMs"** — Apple's December-quarter gross margin was guided to **45–46% from 47.9%** purely on memory inflation, so a slower build is the first line of relief, and it will not show up before fiscal 2028.
2. **The company-news board was genuinely two-sided and genuinely big.** **DUOL +28.47%**, **KD +34.81%**, **XYZ +15.11%**, **TREE +33.43%**, **CRCT +50.34%**, **CMTG +66.67%**, **HELE +27.32%** on the upside; **FIS -24.10%**, **UNM -17.72%**, **MOD -25.41%**, **DOX -29.05%**, **NRIX -39.37%**, **ECG -29.69%**, **CVS -12.21%**, **DDOG -14.42%** on the downside — all on prints, all with relative volume above 2×.
3. **The gap was the trade.** Almost every name that mattered gapped, and almost every gap **held into the bell**: AAPL +2.91% pre-market → +6.03% close, MSFT +4.36% → +6.85%, AMZN +5.63% → +6.74%, and on the other side VRT -7.97% → -13.45%, VST -10.17% → -13.70%, FIX -13.24% → -15.78%. The exceptions are informative: **META gapped +3.24% and closed +1.46%** and **FSLR gapped +10.81% and closed +4.59%** — both faded.

## Method and caveats

- **Prices and volumes are the settled 2026-11-04 regular-session close** from the TradingView scanner re-pull at **16:02:10 ET** (10 minutes after the bell). This is the same source and the same basis as the prior digest, so day-over-day comparison is valid.
- **The bridge's `market_state` is `post` at 21:03–21:04Z and its `price` is therefore the after-hours mark, not the close.** Across 88 names compared side by side, the bridge price differs from the TradingView settled close by a **median 0.375%**, with a **maximum 5.34% (MOD, which bounced after the bell)**; the bridge's volume is a **median ~12% above** the TradingView regular-session volume because it includes post-close prints. **The settled close is therefore taken from TradingView** and the bridge is used only as the cross-check, the `as_of`/`market_state` stamp and the index/macro levels. Publishing the bridge's post-close mark as "the close" would be the error this caveat exists to prevent.
- **RVol denominator:** trailing 10 settled sessions from the same series (2026-10-21 → 2026-11-03), computed market-wide from `polygon-io grouped_daily`. A screener's own RVol column uses a different denominator and is not comparable across sources; where the market-wide denominator was unavailable, the row is flagged in the CSV rather than silently mixed.
- **Catalyst sourcing:** every catalyst line in this digest was read from a dated item (Yahoo news API with `providerPublishTime`, a company press release, an 8-K, or `web_search`) **in this session**. Where a name moved without a catalyst I could source, it is published in Tier B or TECH_WATCH with "no same-session company catalyst" rather than being given a plausible story. **Names where the print was real but the price reaction ran the other way (ECG, SMCI) are labelled as such** rather than being forced into a directional read.
- **Screen completeness:** the trigger screen is `|chg| ≥ 3% OR |gap| ≥ 3%` over 5,592 equities plus 4,676 ETFs; every survivor of the floors was priced (2,048 equities tiered, 257 ETFs in the sector tape). There is no unpriced tail and no top-N cap.
- **Not verified:** the proximate cause of **SWKS**'s -14.2% opening gap, **LMT**'s -3.8% gap, **SKHY**'s move and **EXPE**'s print could not be confirmed with a dated source; those names are published with the price and no catalyst claim. **ECG's** full-day drawdown was verified in price by two independent sources but its cause was not established beyond the data-centre-infrastructure de-rate.
- **The pre-open feed's catalyst column is not usable for this session.** The verdicts in the gap annex come from 5-minute bars, not from the feed.
