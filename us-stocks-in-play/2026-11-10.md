## The headline

**The inflation print broke the relief bid, and the tradeable board got rebuilt from scratch underneath it.** The **October CPI came in hot** — headline **+0.4% m/m** against a +0.3% consensus and core **+0.3%** against +0.2%, and because last year's shutdown left the bureau with no October 2025 base, **no year-over-year figure was published at all**. The 10-year yield jumped to **4.983%** (from 4.864%), the two-year surged about 19bp to **4.74%**, and Fed Governor Christopher Waller — who said *before* the release that a hot print should put a **50bp hike** "on the table" — is now backed by the data: CME FedWatch moved to roughly **85% odds of a 25bp hike and 15% of 50bp** for December 8–9, up from 84% priced on Monday. The market gave back about half of Monday's rally: the **S&P 500 settled -0.38% at 7,520.93**, the **Nasdaq Composite -0.74% at 25,065.88**, the **Dow -0.30% at 52,231.89** and the **Russell 2000 -0.71% at 2,820.55**.

**The caveat that changes how this should be read: the headline index understated the damage.** The **equal-weighted S&P was down only about 0.2%** while the cap-weighted index fell 0.38% — the selling was concentrated in the mega-caps, and **Nvidia alone is the story**. The Information reported that CEO **Jensen Huang's relationship with Alice Huang facilitated the shipment of at least $7.5bn of chips through Chinese firms**, on top of the board's independent investigation and the FBI inquiry the President confirmed; **NVDA closed -5.53% on $71.03bn of turnover** (RVol 2.43), took out the $170 level chart watchers had flagged, and is on track for its **lowest close since March 30**, with an RSI of 23 — the most oversold reading since June 2019 — with **earnings next Tuesday**. **Chip stocks have now lost roughly $5trn of market value since October 6**, about a quarter of their worth, while the typical S&P 500 stock is up about 4% over the same stretch. The **equal-weight line and the cap-weight line disagree, and that disagreement is the day's real finding.**

**The third thing worth saying is that the tradeable end of the board is company-specific and mostly idiosyncratic — and it is split hard by sign.** The **single-stock winners are earnings-and-clinical stories**: **PEPG +92.89%** on Phase 2 FREEDOM2-DM1 data showing **46.5% mean splicing correction, the highest reported in a multiple-dose study**; **TTEC +72.04%** on a Q3 beat, a reaffirmed FY26 outlook and a 10-Q that flags a possible sale of its Digital business; **ZIP +53.15%** on a Q3 beat-and-raise; **AAON +19.31%** on a record quarter that raised FY26 sales growth to 72–75%. The **losers are guidance and capital-structure events, not a macro read-through**: **HIMS -16.56%** on a double miss and an EBITDA cut, **APPS -35.84%** on four points of gross-margin compression despite a revenue beat, **GRPN -23.05%** on a guidance cut, **PAYS -18.94%** after a 10-Q disclosed the loss of 113 plasma centers, **MNTK -57.19%** because it cannot file its 10-Q on time, and **BMGL -64.26%** on a Nasdaq volatility pause over a dilutive offering. **The AI-power complex is the one place where macro and company met**: **GEV -3.92%** after SoftBank disclosed a **$3.73bn loss** on slot fees for US AI data-centre equipment, and the gap **faded** from -8.90% at the open to -3.89% at the bell. **At this slot the gap that held is the signal, and 416 of 943 names held into the bell.**

## Method and caveats

- **Every published price and volume is the SETTLED 2026-11-10 regular-session close and the settled regular-session volume.** The close is the bridge's `market-recap` post-bell regular-session print, cross-checked against the Nasdaq official quote API's post-bell `secondaryData` field; the volume is the bridge's settled session volume.
