## The headline

**Nvidia lost its CEO before the open, and the market did the obvious thing: it bought the names that do not depend on Nvidia's roadmap.** Jensen Huang resigned as President, Chief Executive Officer and a member of the board, effective immediately; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO, Harvey C. Jones is the company's first-ever board chair, and a permanent CEO search is open. NVDA opened **-13.08%** and closed **-4.34%** on **$121.66bn** of turnover — more than the rest of the board combined — and the gap **FADED**, which at this slot means it is not the signal. The rotation is the story: **AMD +5.86% ($31.89bn)** on a William Blair upgrade and a BofA target raise to $700, and **AVGO +5.54% ($15.43bn)** as the custom-silicon alternative. The names Nvidia funds or supplies broke instead: **NBIS -16.84%** on its own print, **CRWV -6.86%**, **SMCI -11.30%**, **SNDK -4.11%**, **MU -1.59%**, **TSM -1.87%**, **DELL -0.64%**.

**The second channel is rates, and it is the reason the leadership shock found a soft tape to land on.** The 10-year yield settled at **5.103%, +10.5bp**, the third straight leg of the hawkish repricing that started with Tuesday's hot CPI print; the dollar index is at **103.17 (+0.26%)**, gold **-2.96% to $3,882**, bitcoin **-3.41% to $71,406**, and WTI **+2.81% to $88.96**. The sector tape follows the curve rather than the chips: **energy is the only green sector (XLE +1.50%)**, while **IWM -1.64% against SPY -0.34%** says the damage was done to the small, rate-sensitive end, and the VIX settled **+7.72% at 18.84**.

**The caveat that changes how this board should be read: this is one event's read-through, and the RVol says the tape was sold rather than hunted.** Of the **1,170** names on the board, **25 are Tier A and 50 Soft-A** — and a large share of that Soft-A tier is the *same* Nvidia shock, not 50 independent stories. The volume ratio settles it: the mega-cap complex is trading **below its own ten-day average** (ORCL 0.38, INTC 0.39, MRVL 0.49, TSM 0.60, MU 0.70, AMAT 0.87), while the names that cleared the Tier A bar are almost all **single-stock prints** — **CDNL 21.5×, ADIG 10.7×, YETI 3.8×, NVDA 3.15×, FRMI 3.20×, LUNR 2.91×, CTVA 2.64×, NBIS 2.66×, TWST 2.22×, SMCI 2.22×**. The tradeable end of today is the idiosyncratic print, not the complex.

**And the gap annex says something the tier tables cannot: the gaps that mattered mostly HELD, while the two gaps the market remembers did not.** **NBIS (-22.47% → -16.78%), CDNL (-49.37% → -62.37%), SMCI (-10.35% → -11.34%), YETI (-7.01% → -10.42%)** kept or extended their opening move into the bell. **NVDA (-13.08% → -4.34%), CRWV (-16.23% → -6.86%) and LUNR (+17.82% → +6.98%)** gave theirs back. Two names **reversed** outright: **FRMI (-6.08% → +13.81%)** and **CLBT (+5.28% → -14.25%)**. **GPN is the cleanest single event of the day and it had no gap at all** — it opened flat and fell 12% at 12:14 ET when Reuters reported that CD&R and GTCR had abandoned their takeover talks.

## Method and caveats

- **Every published price and volume is the SETTLED 2026-11-12 regular-session close and the settled regular-session volume.** The close, the previous close and the session volume are the bridge's `market-recap` post-bell regular-session print, cross-checked against the Nasdaq official quote API's post-bell `secondaryData` field on all 1,170 screened names — **three disagreements above 0.6%**, and Yahoo's daily bar corroborated the bridge on all three. The change percentage is recomputed from the **same** source's close and previous close, so no row mixes a close from one source with a percentage from another.
- **One source per field.** The `polygon-io` bar for the session being published had not consolidated upstream at run time, so the settled session volume is the bridge's, not the scanner's — and the scanner's `volume` column is never published as the numerator. The bridge's post-bell volume is **a floor**: the closing auction consolidates after the bell, and the runtime contract measures the same read running 0.7%-30.2% low on other days.
- **RVol = settled session volume ÷ the trailing-10 settled average from the same series** (the 10 sessions ending 2026-11-11, market-wide from `polygon-io grouped_daily`, 11,993 symbols with a complete denominator). The screener's own RVol column uses a different denominator and is **not** mixed in. **RVol is a soft preference at this slot**, and the RVol ≥ 2.0 Tier A bar is what pushes the mega-cap leaders — ORCL at 0.38, INTC at 0.39, MRVL at 0.49, TSM at 0.60, MU at 0.70 — out of Tier A on a day when they were the biggest names on the board. **That is the finding, not an omission.**
- **The gap verdicts are computed at the bell, not at 15:50.** At the pre-close slot the question is whether a gap *held into the bell*, so the annex verdicts are computed against the **16:00 close** of the reconstructed series, not the 15:50 point. The bands are the shared helper's: `|gap| < 2%` = `no_move`, a sign flip = `reverse`, the move given back = `faded`, otherwise `held`. The `Open` column is the scanner's session-open gap and the path points are the 5-minute series' prints, each relative to the same previous close.
- **Soft-A includes names whose same-session catalyst is a SECTOR event, not a company release.** The Nvidia leadership shock and the DOJ's Super Micro probe are material, same-session and tradeable, so the AI-hardware complex is tiered on them — but every such row says so in words ("no company-specific catalyst; the same-session catalyst is SECTOR"), and no row claims a company event that did not happen.
- **The board is wide by construction and the tradeable end is thin.** 1,170 names cleared the floors, of which **25 are Tier A, 50 Soft-A and 1 Tier C**. The 263 TECH_WATCH and 831 Tier B names are the *beta*, not stories — they are published so the imbalance is visible, not because they are tradeable. **The counts in the tier headings are the full tier counts; the tables are capped.**
- **Two names on this board are not what their ticker suggests.** **XLAB** (Exascale Labs) closed **+118.27%** on **$123m** at an RVol of 66.7× — the day's most extreme mover and a **flow event with no same-session company catalyst located**: the move is a 176.4×-RVOL spike at 14:21 ET on the Compal GPU-server order announced on 2026-11-09, on a ~$67m market cap that is down 61% in ten sessions. **SMX** (Security Matters) closed about **-56%** on a $4.76m drawdown under its $250m SEPA equity line and a 2.8-for-1 reverse split effective 2026-11-09, but it is **not on this board**: after the split it has roughly 0.9m shares, so it fails the $50m market-cap floor. Both are named here rather than left in the CSV.
- **Every row carries its catalyst provenance.** `has_catalyst_timestamp` is `yes` where the catalyst carried a verified timestamp — an 8-K, a dated release, a dated wire — and `no` otherwise, and `reason_without_timestamp` carries the **reason** on every one of the 1170 rows — including the explicit "no same-session company catalyst located" default on names where a search was run and found nothing, and the read-through wording on the sympathy names, so a re-tier can never wipe the reasons that were researched.
- **Where a print landed today but its figures were not sourced, the row says exactly that.** 56 names on this board reported today on the TradingView earnings calendar; for those whose EPS and revenue were sourced this run the row carries the numbers, and for the rest the row carries the release timestamp and an explicit statement that the figures were not sourced, rather than a number nobody checked.
- **The tier tables are rendered from the same dataset the CSV is written from**, so a number cannot be right in one file and wrong in the other.
- **This board is a reconstruction of a live session, not a live board.** Every number is settled; the post-market brief owns the after-close earnings reactions, and this digest's `overnight_carry` hands the watch list forward rather than re-telling it.
