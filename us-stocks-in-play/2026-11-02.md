## The headline

**This was not Friday's sell-off repeating. It was a violent internal rotation inside it, and two single-name events did almost all of the work.** Crude collapsed **-7.7%** on Iran–Qatar diplomacy hopes, the S&P 500 ended **-0.47%** after being up more than 1% intraday, and the *composition* changed completely: **semis and software sold off hard while energy, airlines, cruise and consumer defensives bid**. The small-cap tape was flat (IWM -0.05%) and the defensive sectors were up 1.6–2.4%, while the AI complex — the market's leadership — was being sold.

The caveat that changes how this should be read: **the marginal dollar did not leave the market, it left AI.** Every large-cap with real news broke *down* — **MRVL -12.75% on $9.6bn**, **ORCL -8.79% on $6.3bn**, **INTC -8.80% on $13.5bn**, **LITE -18.65% on $9.8bn**, **AMD -7.55% on $13.1bn**, **MU -5.78% on $22.9bn** — while the names that were green were green on **oil** (airlines, cruise) or on **their own prints** (IDXX, TEM, TATT, INO). The tradeable names today carried *company* information. The read-across names were carrying the sector's beta.

Two things are genuinely new information, and they are the reason this is not just a repeat of Friday:

1. **Marvell agreed to buy Lumentum in a $115bn all-stock deal — and the market sold both stocks, with the TARGET selling harder than the acquirer.** MRVL closed **-12.75%** and LITE **-18.65%**. At the 4.500x exchange ratio against MRVL's own $171.35 close, LITE's implied value was **$771.07** — and LITE closed at **$683.92**, i.e. **11.3% BELOW the deal's implied value**. **A deal whose target trades 11% below its own implied value is not a bid the market believes; it is a spread.**
2. **The oil shock was a genuine macro break, not noise.** WTI fell from $104.12 to **$96.06 (-7.74%)** as Secretary of State Rubio confirmed Trump prefers diplomacy over force against Iran, with Qatari mediation continuing for a third day. The pre-midterm risk premium in crude was being unwound in a single session, and that repriced an entire cost line — jet fuel — within hours.

## Method and caveats

- **Two-phase pre-close procedure, as the skill requires.** Phase 1 (discovery, the universe, the floors, the intraday path, the gap annex, catalyst research) was run live between 15:50 and 15:58 ET. Phase 2 (settled re-price of every tiered name, settled session volume, recomputed RVol on the settled volume) was run after the 16:00 ET bell, with the bridge pull at 16:01:50 ET and the scanner's universe re-pulled at 16:22 ET once its post-bell consolidation had finished. **A scanner snapshot taken at 16:06 ET was still mid-settlement and disagreed with the bridge by a median 0.4% (up to 2.4%); it was discarded and re-pulled.** **The numbers published here are settled. The gap-annex `now%` column is the intraday print at the time the helper ran, not the settled close, and is labelled as such.**
- **Screens and floors.** Price ≥ $1; market cap ≥ $50M (soft); session $ volume ≥ $2M; RVol a **soft preference, not a hard gate**, per the US skill. **Trigger = `|chg| ≥ 3%` OR `|gap| ≥ 3%`** — screened on the trigger, not the close-to-close move, so gap-and-fade names are not silently dropped. **The $2M floor was applied to 1,292 names and all 1,292 were priced** — there is no unpriced tail, and no cap was applied.
- **Price source.** Bridge `market-recap` for 708 names, the TradingView scanner's own settled fields for 584, and Nasdaq `secondaryData` for the 18 index/sector ETFs. Both equity sources are Yahoo-sourced. **The price source for every row is recorded in the CSV** (`price_source`). On the 720 names where both sources could be compared, they agreed within 0.1% on 59.4%; the residual is concentrated in thin names and wide spreads, and **the bridge is preferred wherever it exists**.
- **RVol basis.** Session volume ÷ the **trailing 10 settled sessions from the same historical series**, for 199 names. For the rest the TradingView `relative_volume_10d_calc` column is used and flagged `tradingview_relative_volume_10d_calc` in the CSV. **The two denominators differ and are never mixed within a row**; a screener's own RVol column is not comparable across sources.
- **Tier rules used.** Tier A = trigger + liquidity floors + RVol ≥ 2.0 + material same-session catalyst. Soft-A = trigger + floors + high-material same-session catalyst + RVol < 2.0 (known soft). TECH_WATCH = trigger + floors + strong absolute turnover + soft RVol + no same-session catalyst. Tier B = trigger + floors with no or weak catalyst. Tier C = buyout gap watch, deal-spread, credit distress or capital-structure event. **Buyout rule applied strictly:** EVGO, PERI and LITE are Tier C despite being among the day's largest movers, because a fresh deal, a tight collar and a below-implied-value spread are spread trades, not momentum chases.
- **Could not be verified.** LULD / limit status for any name (both halt feeds are dead). **The catalyst tape is absent**, so no name carries a verified catalyst *timestamp*. **SEC 8-K item codes.** Own-series trailing-10 volume for 36 names. **The specific catalysts for the 24 TECH_WATCH names and the bulk of Tier B** — those rows are explicitly labelled "no catalyst verified" or "read-across" and are not dressed up as information.
- **Low float.** Shares-outstanding was not pulled this run; the screener's fundamental field was found unreliable in the prior digest and SEC was not queried. **No low-float flag is asserted in this digest**, and the four Tier C / flagged names (ULH $4.9M, NVA $10.1M, TATT $19.9M, NFE $2.6M) are flagged on **turnover**, which is measured.
- **The gap annex's `now%` is intraday, not settled.** For example EL's annex row reads -4.21% (the helper's last bar) against a settled close of -4.22%. These agree to 0.01pp where the helper ran late, but the annex is a path, not a close.
- **Nothing here is a recommendation.** This is a scan of what moved and why, with the catalyst attached.
