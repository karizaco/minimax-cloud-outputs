## The headline

**A short-seller report broke the AI trade and a record IPO could not hold it: the S&P 500 fell -2.02% to 7,175.83, its sixth straight down session, the Nasdaq fell -2.25% and the VIX jumped 13.64% to 27.91.** The trigger was Hunterbrook Media's 11:24 ET report on **AMD** — the outlet disclosed that its affiliated fund, Hunterbrook Capital, had gone **short AMD**, and alleged that AMD's firmware kept reaching blacklisted Chinese server maker IEIT Systems, that AMD bills over 20% of revenue to China and Hong Kong, and that it depends on a Chinese packaging partner. AMD denied the claims as a misrepresentation and said it complies with US export controls. **AMD opened +2.27% and closed -7.72% on $34.6bn** — the largest turnover on the board — and Reuters had the verdict: *Anthropic's debut failed to revive AI trade optimism*. The 10-year barely moved and the dollar was flat, so this was an AI-complex unwind, not a rates or a dollar story.

**The shape of the day is the finding: almost everything that gapped up gave the gap back.** Anthropic's debut was the catalyst for the opening bid — priced at $83, above its $70-78 range, the largest IPO on record at $81.3bn — and the shares opened at $111.50 shortly before noon, briefly took the market cap above $2trn, and settled **$97.17, +17.07% on the IPO price but 14.5% below their first print**, on **847.3m shares**. The complex that read across to it reversed instead: **Micron gapped +4.97% and closed -2.10%**, **SanDisk gapped +4.54% and closed -2.71%**, **Nebius gapped +4.33% and closed -9.39%**, **CoreWeave gapped +4.56% and closed -6.10%**, **Alphabet gapped +4.20% and closed -3.46%**. **220 of the board's names reversed**, against 88 that held their gap into the bell — the single most tradeable fact on this board is how rarely an opening gap survived today.

**The tradeable end was the idiosyncratic print, and it was almost all downside.** **Seabridge Gold -29.32% on a 7.37x RVol** as the B.C. Supreme Court's quashing of the KSM mine's substantial start determination continued to work, **StoneX -26.71%** on a fiscal Q4 EPS miss, **Liquidity Services -24.88%** on a Q4 revenue decline and a soft Q1 guide, **Williams-Sonoma -15.16%** on an in-line print with a maintained guide, **NetEase -11.39%** on a Q3 revenue miss, **Jumia -8.76%** on a guidance break. Against that, **Palo Alto Networks +6.42%** (the fiscal Q1 FY2027 beat-and-raise with the $4.5bn Applied Compute acquisition), **PDD +11.04%**, **Valvoline +10.40%**, and **Insmed +20.58%** — the biggest mover on the board, on a report that **Sanofi is weighing an acquisition**, with no price, no terms and no agreement, which the routine's own no-chase rule puts in **Tier C** rather than in the momentum tiers.

## Method and caveats

- **Every number on this board is rendered from ONE dataset** (`2026-11-19-candidates.csv`, 1,085 rows), so the board and the CSV cannot disagree. The tables are generated from it, not hand-typed.
- **The prices are settled and the volumes are settled — after a measured correction.** The first post-bell read understated the session volume by a median 1.93%; the published numbers are the re-read, taken after two consecutive whole-set pulls agreed, and cross-checked against the Nasdaq official tape at a median volume ratio of 1.00000. **A reader comparing this board with any earlier board published on the same day must use the settled figures, not the first post-bell print.**
- **RVol is a soft preference at this slot, not a hard gate.** The Tier A bar is RVol ≥ 2.0; every other tier is reachable on RVol below 1. The denominator is the trailing 10 settled sessions ending 2026-11-18, computed market-wide from `polygon-io grouped_daily`.
- **The gap verdicts are computed at the BELL (16:00), not at 15:50.** The open board's own gap annex scores the open-to-09:50 window; this board scores open-to-bell on the settled close, and the two verdicts are not interchangeable.
- **A gap that held into the bell is the signal at this slot; a gap that faded is not.** `held` means the opening move was still largely intact at 16:00; `reverse` means the opening move flipped sign; `faded` means it was given back; `no_move` means the open was inside the 2% noise band and there is no gap to read.
- **A Tier A bar is volume behind a company event, not a good outcome.** Most of today's Tier A names are prints that went the wrong way. A near-offer or a media-reported deal is never promoted to Tier A — **Insmed is Tier C for exactly that reason** — and a capital-structure print is a watch, not a chase.
- **A sector or macro event is a soft catalyst and never a Tier A company catalyst.** That is why **UnitedHealth sits in Soft-A on a 3.11x RVol** and the megacaps are here at RVol near 1.
- **`no same-session company catalyst located` is the honest default on a row where a search was run and found nothing** — it is not a claim that nothing happened, and it is set as the default in the tiering step so a re-tier cannot wipe a researched reason.
- **Anthropic is on this board by hand, with its basis disclosed.** A first session has no prior close and no ten-session average, so its change is measured against the $83 IPO price, its RVol is not computable and its gap verdict is not applicable. **Its change and RVol are not comparable with the rest of the board.**
- **The open board's pace column and this board's RVol column are never comparable** — different quantities on different denominators. The arbitration table above carries the open board's two pace bases beside the settled RVol; the settled number settles the volume, and the pace stays on the open board as a screen against the 0.125 constant, whose regime band spans 10.77%–16.49%.
- **Degraded sources: 2** — (1) the Nasdaq official tape disagrees with the published Yahoo close by more than 0.6% on 31 names, and the disagreement is disclosed above rather than silently resolved; (2) `preopen_feed.py` ran but its verdict column is scored against a 15:50 read, so the gap verdicts here are recomputed at the bell from the same 5-minute series, and the ntrt gap brief for 2026-11-19 was not published. Finviz is egress-blocked and was not needed for any published number, so it is not counted; the scanner's `halted` column is null and the halt state is **not measurable**, which is a property of the data rather than a source failure.
- **The halt state is not measurable this session** — the scanner's `halted` column is null on every row — so no halt count is published anywhere in this digest.
- **Source hygiene:** the Yahoo daily and 5-minute endpoints answered only with a browser User-Agent; the plain request is throttled from this egress. The TradingView trigger screen was pulled as four complete single calls, because the universe exceeds the scanner's own page cap and a truncated pull fails silently.
