## The headline

**The character of the day did not change — the prices did.** The midday digest called this an AI-capex unwind with a rotation under it; the close confirms it and took it further. SPY closed **-1.81%**, QQQ **-3.51%**, the semis **SOXX -8.24% / SMH -6.98%** and tech **XLK -4.89%**, while the defensive bid stayed intact all afternoon — **XLP +1.41%, XLE +1.88%, XLU +0.72%, XLV +0.43%** and **TLT +0.43%**. VIX closed **20.31 (+18.1%)** after touching **23.93** intraday. That is still a rotation, not a liquidation: the equal-weight tape was green while the cap-weight index was down.

The caveat that most changes how this reads: **the marginal dollar is still leaving semiconductors, not the market.** Every mega-cap with a real catalyst broke *down* — INTC **-28.58%**, RDDT **-29.27%**, TWLO **-15.13%**, ILMN **-18.64%**, RBLX **-18.07%** — and the two biggest names in the book that stayed green did so for idiosyncratic reasons rather than market ones (GPN on a takeover report, REGN on a Sanofi accounting change). The names with genuinely new information are the tradeable ones; the read-across names are momentum, not information.

Two developments broke **after** the midday screen was taken, and they are the main reason this close digest is not just a repeat:

1. **Global Payments +13.17% ($1.62B)** — the FT reported at 12:51 PM ET that **CD&R is pursuing a ~$40bn take-private** of GPN. Under the skill's buyout rule a fresh deal with no disclosed terms is a **Tier C gap watch, not a momentum chase**, so GPN is filed in Tier C despite being the 5th-largest dollar turnover on the board.
2. **The SpaceX-mobile cost argument broke mid-session** — telecom analyst William Webb put the cost of SpaceX entering US wireless at **$120–130bn** beyond its ~$28bn of spectrum deals, and T-Mobile's CEO ruled out an MVNO deal. **TMUS +6.10%** ($1.29B) and **SPCX -6.37%** ($16.2B) are the two ends of that trade, and SPCX is the 3rd-largest turnover on the board.

Third, the afternoon was **not** a simple continuation: **GDYN extended 6.3pp to +39.78%**, **CVEO +6.8pp to +16.50%**, **ACCO +6.5pp to +24.54%**, **NFE +13.5pp to +30.34%** — while **RMNI faded 5.3pp** from +29.82% to +24.56% and **CRMD deteriorated 5.4pp** to -25.33%. The buyers were still buying the earnings beats at the close.

## Method and caveats

- **Slot and floors.** 15:51 ET fire, settled after the 16:00 ET close. Floors applied at the midday/close scale: price >= $1, market cap >= $50M (soft), session $ volume >= ~$2M; RVol a **soft preference, not a hard gate**, per the US skill.
- **Price source.** Settled regular-session close from Nasdaq `secondaryData` for 38 equities and 13 ETFs; the StockTwits bridge daily bar (captured 15:57 ET) for the remainder. **Reconciliation:** the two agreed within 0.1% on 19 of 34 names; the widest gaps were INTC (+1.07% Nasdaq) and ATEC (+1.37%), and the bridge's 15:57 volume ran **10–33% below** the settled Nasdaq volume on the largest names. Nasdaq was preferred wherever it was available, and every tiered row's `price_source` is recorded in the CSV.
- **RVol basis.** Session volume ÷ the **trailing 10 settled sessions** from the same historical series. The denominator was verified **byte-identical** to the midday digest's Nasdaq series on all 21 names checked (ratio 1.00 to six decimal places), so RVol here is directly comparable with the 12:36 ET figures. The only difference is the numerator: the session volume at the close rather than at midday.
- **Tier rules used.** Tier A = trigger met + $ volume >= $5M + RVol >= 2.0 + material same-session catalyst. Soft-A = same, with RVol < 2.0 (known soft). TECH_WATCH = trigger met + $ volume >= $10M + RVol 1.0–2.0 + no same-session catalyst. Tier B = trigger met + $ volume >= $2M with no or weak catalyst, or a low-float demotion. Tier C = buyout gap watch, credit distress, or an extreme move with no confirmed catalyst. "Trigger" = day move >= 5% **or** open gap >= 3%. **Buyout rule applied:** GPN is Tier C, not momentum, because no deal terms are public.
- **Could not be verified.** LULD / limit status for any name. The detailed FMC figures — an automated summary circulating claims an 80% revenue decline and a $2.07bn GAAP net loss, which is not credible against FMC's ~$2.4bn quarterly revenue and is almost certainly a parse error, so FMC is filed at Tier B with the anomaly called out. The specific catalysts for **COUR, CRMD, RGEN, APP, VRRM, NFE** and the NRXP trailing-10 denominator. SEC 8-K item codes.
- **Low float.** Shares-outstanding came from the screener's fundamental field, which is **not reliable** — it read ATEC at 154.5m against a true count near 75m — and could not be checked against SEC (403). Flags are therefore applied only where the field read under 20m: **CVCO 7.7m, CACC 10.4m, CVEO 10.3m, NFE 16.3m**, plus **ULH (6.98m float)** from a third-party screener. All five were **flagged and not auto-promoted**; CACC, CVEO, CVCO, NFE and ULH sit in Tier B rather than Tier A.
- **Where this run differs from the midday digest.** GPN, AZN, SYK, GDDY, FMC, SPOT, COUR, CACC, APP, RGEN and SPCX were absent from the 12:36 ET screen. RVol is higher across the board because the session volume kept building; names whose RVol crossed 2.0 on the close moved up a tier (BFAM 8.22x, ATEC 8.05x, RDDT 5.62x, GDYN 5.19x, ACCO 5.85x).
- **Nothing here is a recommendation.** This is a scan of what moved and why, with the catalyst attached.
