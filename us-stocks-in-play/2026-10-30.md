## The headline

This was **one story, not a hundred**: the AI-capex complex broke. Intel's Q4 guide came in below the Street on a **PC demand collapse driven by memory prices**, and because Intel is a demand *caller* rather than a supplier, every company downstream of enterprise and consumer hardware got repriced with it — semis first, then the AI-infrastructure renters, then the software that sells into them. The transmission order is visible in the tape: SOXX -8.4%, SMH -6.9%, then DELL/CRWV/NET/DDOG/ALAB/ARM/CRDO all in the -10% to -12% range on volume that was **light relative to their own 10-day norms** (RVol 0.4–1.2x on most). Amazon gapped -5.8% and got bought back to -2.0% on 1.42x RVol — the only mega-cap that fought the tape.

The caveat that most changes how this reads: **almost none of the damage is a fresh company-specific shock.** MU, NVDA, SNDK, WDC, STX, LRCX, AMAT, ANET, TSEM, AXTI, AAOI and COHR all moved without their own news — they were dragged by INTC's PC commentary and by the memory-pricing debate it opened. On a tape like that, the individual names with genuine same-session catalysts (INTC, RDDT, REGN, CDNA, OSPN, GDYN, BFAM, ATEC) are the tradeable ones and the read-across names are momentum, not information.

Two more things worth flagging. First, **the selloff is narrow, not systemic**: SPY -1.50% against QQQ -3.13%, with staples +1.70%, energy +1.32%, utilities +0.97% and healthcare +0.96% all green — a violent **rotation**, not a liquidation. Second, a **second, unrelated shock ran underneath the tech tape**: the record **$6.3B Binance hack** (North Korea's TraderTraitor unit, per TRM Labs) crushed the crypto complex — BTC -4.9%, COIN -12.5%, MSTR -6.3%, HOOD -8.1%, CRCL -8.4% — and simultaneously forced **gold and silver into liquidation** (GLD -3.1%, SLV -6.0%, GDX -5.1%) as hot September PCE and a fourth straight monthly gain in crude oil pushed December Fed-hike odds to ~70%. Those are two different trades that happened to share a tape.

## Method and caveats

- **These are live intraday prices at 12:36-12:41 ET, not settled closes.** The session is still open with ~3h20m to the bell. Every figure moves; this digest is a snapshot, not a closing statement. Unlike the 10-29 close digest, no price here should be treated as final.
- **The TradingView and Yahoo MCPs named in the skill do not exist in this sandbox**, and Yahoo's HTTP APIs are 429-throttled and Finviz is Cloudflare-403. The entire data path is HTTP-based. Nasdaq's public APIs and stockanalysis.com were used instead. This is a **degraded but not failed** run: the substitute path delivered live quotes, intraday bars, prior closes, session volume, trailing-10 volume history and market cap for every name in the digest.
- **The Nasdaq bulk screener was verified stale and excluded from pricing.** It returned a previous session's prices under an HTTP 200. Every price here comes either from the Nasdaq intraday chart endpoint or the stockanalysis quote API, and the two were cross-checked against each other on all 87 tiered names.
- **Nasdaq's chart endpoint includes premarket bars from 04:00 ET.** Open gaps, day ranges and close-position figures were computed from the **09:30-16:00 ET window only**. Two independent definitions of "open" disagree slightly (first RTH print vs the official opening print) — a handful of names differ by 1-6% on that field alone, which is why INTC's open gap reads -26.10% from the 09:30 bar against the ~-25.7% official open.
- **RVol is the trailing 10 settled sessions from the same Nasdaq historical series.** 227 of 227 names with an RVol have a trailing-10 denominator; the Nasdaq `AverageVolume` field was used only where historical returned fewer than 10 bars, and is labelled `nasdaq_avg` in the working data. **A mid-session RVol is structurally understated** — the denominator is a full day and the numerator is a partial day — so every RVol here understates the full-session ratio by roughly the fraction of the session elapsed. It is still directionally valid for comparing names against their own norms, which is what tiering uses it for.
- **Tier A mixes two different things, deliberately.** INTC, AMZN, RDDT, COIN, ATEC, BFAM, VRRM and SNDK carry genuine same-session catalysts (INTC's guide, AMZN's miss and holiday guide, Reddit's traffic collapse, Coinbase's revenue miss plus the Binance hack, Alphatec's miss and guidance cut, Bright Horizons' impairment and guidance cut, Verra Mobility's CEO change, Sandisk's pricing-peak selloff). **The other large-cap names in Tier A — MU, NVDA, AMD, LITE, MRVL, SKHY, DELL, NBIS, BE, CRWV, STX, LRCX, COHR, AAOI, QCOM — carry a catalyst that is either a read-across or a sector event, not their own news.** They are ranked by dollar volume because they are what moved, but they are not the same kind of trade as INTC.
- **On a tape this correlated, individual catalysts get overwhelmed by the macro driver.** In the 10-29 digest six of the largest single-name dislocations were guidance events inside a calm index. Today the index itself was moving (-1.50% SPY, -3.13% QQQ, VIX +16.7%), so the marginal information in any single print is lower than usual. The tape rewards *relative* strength, which is why AMZN (+0.6pp against SPY), REGN, CBOE, MEOH, EXEL, GDYN and ULH stand out.
- **ARI's apparent -63.6% is a dividend artifact, not a move.** ARI's $4.10 quarterly dividend went ex-date today; the raw prior close of $6.45 is not comparable to the $2.35 dividend-adjusted reference. On an adjusted basis the stock is flat. It is not a mover and is listed in Tier C only to prevent a false read.
- **UCFI was excluded entirely** — the only quote available for it carried a 2025-10-01 date, so it is a stale bar rather than a live session, despite appearing in the live screener universe.
- **Halt status is unverified for every name** (see Halt watch).
- **No float data was available in this run**, so the skill's low-float flag could not be applied on evidence. Names where float is a genuine risk (AIOK at $100M market cap on $188M turnover, NRXP at $90M cap on 13.26x RVol, NFE at $70M cap, ULH/RMNI on $2-4M turnover) are called out inline instead.
- **The digest is not a close.** A later run of this cron on the same date may publish a different, settled set of prices; if it does, that file supersedes this one for closing purposes.
