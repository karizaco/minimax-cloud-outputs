- **Session state:** US cash session closed. Thursday, not a US exchange holiday. Data is the official RTH close — no after-hours bar is mixed into any table.
- **Slot:** close-of-session digest. Liquidity floors applied at the midday scale (session $ volume >= ~$2-5M; RVol a soft preference, not a hard gate).
- **Sort note:** Tier A -> Soft-A -> TECH_WATCH -> B -> C. **Within each tier, ranked by session USD dollar volume descending.** Tier A and Soft-A are additionally required to carry a material same-session catalyst; TECH_WATCH carries none.
- **Universe scanned:** 157 US-listed names (NYSE/NASDAQ/AMEX, market cap > $50M, price > $1) pulled from a TradingView scanner pass and cross-checked against the Yahoo chart API.
- **RVol basis:** session volume divided by the **trailing 10 settled sessions from the same Yahoo series**. TradingView's `average_volume_10d_calc` was **not** used as the denominator — it disagrees materially and inconsistently with a plain trailing-10 average (TV/Yahoo ratio 0.91x on MU, 1.33x on GOOGL, 2.87x on ADT), so TV-based RVol is systematically unreliable here. Yahoo-basis RVol is the more conservative (higher) number and is what drives tiering.

## The headline

This was a **rotation day, not a risk-off day**. SPY fell only 0.37% and the VIX *fell* 1.83% to 16.60, while IWM was up 0.97% — but underneath that calm index tape, the market repriced the AI capital-expenditure complex hard: Alphabet's Q3 miss and negative free cash flow, plus Microsoft's cut to its calendar-2026 capex outlook, de-rated software, semiconductors and memory (**XLK -1.19%, SOXX -1.55%**) while the money went into healthcare, financials and industrials (**XLV +2.42%, XLF +1.39%, XLI +0.96%**).

The most important caveat for reading this digest: **the biggest single-name dislocations were not macro at all — they were guidance events.** ServiceNow, Alnylam, Iovance, Sprouts, Fortinet and Haleon each fell on their own forward guidance, and Alphabet's miss was about the *shape of 2027 spending* rather than a collapse in demand. The macro driver set the backdrop and the sector rotation; the company-level catalysts set the actual prices. Two of the most violent moves (Alnylam -25.1%, Iovance -35.5%) had *better-than-expected* reported quarters and still closed near their lows, which is a forward-looking market, not a panicked one.

## Method and caveats

- **Prices are official RTH closes.** The Yahoo chart API's last daily bar was verified to carry the 2026-10-29 date for every symbol before being used, and the prior session's bar supplied the change base. Nothing here is an after-hours or intraday quote.
- **RVol denominator is Yahoo's own trailing 10 settled sessions**, not TradingView's field. The two disagree by -9% to +187% depending on the name, and TV's version understates RVol on the large caps (GOOGL 4.1x on TV vs 4.8x on the Yahoo basis). Using the higher, stricter number is what pushes ADT, ALNY, SFM and IOVA into Tier A. Anyone re-running this with a TV denominator will get different tiers.
- **Dollar volume, not share count, drives the sort** — which is why MU ($17.8B on 19.0M shares, i.e. 0.7x volume) is a Tier B name despite being the third-largest dollar turnover on the board, while ALNY ($2.0B on 11.0M shares) is Tier A.
- **Catalyst dates were re-verified against the session.** Several widely-circulated "top movers" pages dated 29 Oct were recycling **28 Oct** moves; every catalyst in this file was pinned to the actual reporting session (for example BHC's 28 Oct close of $6.92 -> $8.885 today; ADT's 27 Oct close of $6.87 vs 28 Oct's $7.41).
- **One reporting-source conflict is unresolved:** CNBC describes ServiceNow's Q4 guide as roughly in line with consensus while 24/7 Wall St describes it as below a ~$4.36B consensus. Both agree on the number that matters — Q4 cRPO growth guided down to 18.5% from 21% constant currency — so that is what the digest reports.
- **Halt status is unverified for every name** (see Halt watch).
- **Excluded from the universe:** GOOGM and GOOGN (Alphabet depositary shares — warrant-type instruments, excluded under the warrants/units rule; GOOG itself is merged into the GOOGL line as the more liquid dual class). **MAPLE** was also dropped: Maplebear now trades as **CART**, and MAPLE returns 404 from Yahoo and "Symbol not exists" from Nasdaq — a ticker-identity trap that would otherwise have produced a fabricated row. Leveraged/inverse ETFs, warrants, rights, units, preferreds and illiquid OTC names are excluded by construction.
- **Not a chase list.** ADT closed 1.3% below its cash consideration and Maplebear traded near the $65 offer; both are arbitrage situations, not momentum.
