## The headline

**Anthropic's second session broke the AI trade: the S&P 500 fell -3.70% to 6,910.39 — a seventh straight down session and a correction — the Nasdaq fell -5.28% to 22,617.73, and the VIX jumped 36.53% to 38.83.** The record IPO that was supposed to re-rate the AI trade did the opposite: **Anthropic priced at $83 above its $70-78 range, opened at $111.50 and closed its debut +17.07% at $97.17 — and on day two the underwriters exercised the green shoe in full, selling 147m more shares at the $83 offer price**, with the stock trading down to $83.10, within $0.10 of the offer, before settling at **$88.91, -8.50% on the debut close and still +7.1% above the offer price**, on 394.6m shares and $35.1bn — the second-largest turnover on the board. **The second ingredient was the Fed, and the bond market disagreed with the equity market about it.** Philadelphia Fed's Anna Paulson signalled that another rate hike may be needed to contain inflation, and CME FedWatch was pricing a **98.4% probability of a December hike** — yet the **10-year yield fell about 14bp to 4.91% and TLT rose +1.37%**. So the selloff was not a rates story: it was a growth-and-duration story, the AI complex repricing what AI capital spending is worth, and the money went into the defensive corners — **staples were the only S&P sector up (+0.71%), healthcare was flat (-0.08%) and the dollar did not move (-0.04%)**. **That split is the finding of the day: an AI-duration unwind with a defensive bid underneath it, not an inflation panic.**

Against that, **Canadian Solar had no gap to read (-1.50%) and still closed -24.94%** — the strategic-alternatives and possible Chapter 11 report landed at 14:26 ET, and the stock fell all afternoon.

**The tradeable end was the idiosyncratic print, and it was almost all downside.** **Canadian Solar -24.94%** on a report that it and its Recurrent Energy subsidiary are working with advisers on strategic alternatives — asset sales, financing and, if necessary, a Chapter 11 filing, on top of a Q3 miss and a 40.9% short interest; **Ellington Credit -22.63%** after an adjusted NII miss and a **37.5% distribution cut** to $0.05 a month; **Savara -18.23%** on the FDA saying it will not act on the molgramostim BLA by the November 22 PDUFA date; **Belite Bio -15.65%** on a three-month FDA review extension that pushes tinlarebant's action date to May 12, 2027; and **Anthropic -8.50%** on $35.1bn. ## Method and caveats

- **Every number on this board is rendered from ONE dataset** (`2026-11-20-candidates.csv`, 1,429 rows), so the board and the CSV cannot disagree. The tables are generated from it, not hand-typed.
- **The prices are settled and the volumes are settled — after a measured correction, and the correction is bigger than on any previous day.** The 15:54 ET pre-bell read understated the settled session volume by a median **17.7%**, and even the 16:01 read was 11.4% low against the Nasdaq tape. The published numbers are the 16:17 whole-set read, which matches the Nasdaq official tape at a median volume ratio of **1.000000** and a median close difference of **0.000003%**. **A reader comparing this board with any earlier board published on the same day must use the settled figures, not the first post-bell print.**
- **RVol is a soft preference at this slot, not a hard gate.** The Tier A bar is RVol ≥ 2.0; every other tier is reachable on RVol below 1. The denominator is the trailing 10 settled sessions ending 2026-11-19, computed market-wide from `polygon-io grouped_daily`.
- **The gap verdicts are computed at the BELL (16:00), not at 15:50.** The open board's own gap annex scores the open-to-09:50 window; this board scores open-to-bell on the settled close, and the two verdicts are not interchangeable.
- **A gap that held into the bell is the signal at this slot; a gap that faded is not.** `held` means the opening move was still largely intact at 16:00; `reverse` means the opening move flipped sign; `faded` means it was given back; `no_move` means the open was inside the 2% noise band and there is no gap to read.
- **A Tier A bar is volume behind a company event, not a good outcome.** All 5 of today's Tier A names are prints that went the wrong way. A near-offer or a media-reported deal is never promoted to Tier A, and a capital-structure print is a watch, not a chase — **Applied Optoelectronics is Tier C for exactly that reason**, on its $550m equity-distribution facility.
- **A sector or macro event is a soft catalyst and never a Tier A company catalyst.** That is why **Nvidia sits in Soft-A on a 1.55x RVol** and the megacaps are here at RVol near 1: the complex broke around them, not because of them.
- **`no same-session company catalyst located` is the honest default on a row where a search was run and found nothing** — it is not a claim that nothing happened, and it is set as the default in the tiering step so a re-tier cannot wipe a researched reason.
- **Anthropic's change is measured against its $97.17 debut close, not the $83 offer price**, and its RVol is not computable — one prior session is not a ten-session average. **Its RVol is not comparable with the rest of the board**, and the $35.1bn it traded is the reason it is on it.
- **The open board's pace column and this board's RVol column are never comparable** — different quantities on different denominators. The arbitration table above carries the open board's two pace bases beside the settled RVol; the settled number settles the volume, and the pace stays on the open board as a screen against the 0.125 constant, whose regime band spans 10.77%–16.49%.
- **Degraded sources: 3** — (1) the Nasdaq official tape disagrees with the published Yahoo close by more than 0.6% on 32 names, and the disagreement is disclosed above rather than silently resolved; (2) the Nasdaq sweep errored on 311 of 1,943 requests, so the cross-check covers 1,629 names rather than the whole set; (3) the ntrt gap brief for 2026-11-20 was not published, so no pre-market reason was available from it. Finviz is egress-blocked and was not needed for any published number, so it is not counted; the scanner's `halted` column is null and the halt state is **not measurable**, which is a property of the data rather than a source failure.
- **The halt state is not measurable this session** — the scanner's `halted` column is null on every row — so no halt count is published anywhere in this digest.
- **Source hygiene:** the Yahoo daily and 5-minute endpoints answered only with a browser User-Agent; the plain request is throttled from this egress. The TradingView trigger screen was pulled as four complete single calls, because the universe exceeds the scanner's own page cap and a truncated pull fails silently.
