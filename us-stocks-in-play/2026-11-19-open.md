## The pace basis — what this board's pace is measured against

**`pace` and the pre-close slot's `RVol` are different quantities on different denominators, and they are never comparable.** The pace column here is a screening measure against the **0.125** opening-share constant; the ten daily medians behind that constant span **10.77%–16.49%**, so a published pace of 1.9 is really 1.8–2.3 on another session's regime. The pre-close slot of this session publishes the settled `RVol` for the same names and is the arbiter of the basis.

**The volume-basis factor is measured on THIS session, on a common window.** Putting the 09:50 numerator on the daily-bar scale needs the ratio of the two series, and that ratio has to be measured over the SAME window, so the engine fetches the 5-minute series and the daily bar for a name back to back and divides one by the other. **388 of the 430 published names are on the session's own same-window ratio** and **42 keep the archived prior-session factor** because their own same-window measurement was unusable — a glitching 5-minute bar, or a daily bar lagging the tape — which is flagged in the candidates CSV rather than smoothed over. **99 names had the archived factor repaired onto the session's own basis** (a disagreement of more than 20%, the archive's corruption signature). The archived prior-session pace is published beside the session's own in the candidates CSV so the pre-close slot can settle the basis against a settled number.

**Two data defects were found and repaired on this run, and both are named rather than smoothed over.**

1. **The BITO reverse-split artifact (a name removed from the board).** ProShares implemented a **1-for-2 reverse split on BITO effective prior to the market open on 2026-11-19** (ProShares press release 2026-11-03; MIAX corporate action alert 2026-11-18). The engine's prior close is the pre-split price and today's price is the post-split price, so the raw print reads **+104.21 percent** and the raw gap reads **+103.97 percent** — **that is the split, not a trade.** The prior close was re-based on the post-split basis, the trigger and the floors re-applied, and the name **dropped out of the published set on the trigger**: BITO was +2.1 percent on the split-adjusted basis, below the 3 percent line. Publishing the raw +104 percent would have put a mechanical corporate action at the top of the board's mover list.
2. **The 5-minute volume cap (a name's pace repaired).** A 5-minute bar cannot plausibly carry 8x the symbol's own median RTH 5-minute bar volume across the 10-day window — the rule the skill states for the Nvidia bar glitch of 2026-11-12, where an unrepaired bar published a pace of 23.29 and a $107.9bn turnover. **FOCL, HYDR** carried a single opening print of **3,906,453 shares against a median RTH bar volume of 19,307** — more than 200x — on a 10-day average of 116,900 shares, which produced a published pace of 269.84 against a 3.71 percent move. The bars were capped at 8x the median and the session volume recomputed: the pace reads **13.05** on 190,659 shares and $0.6m of turnover, and the name's tier is decided on the repaired number. A second name on the same defect fell below the $500,000 dollar floor on the capped volume and **left the published set**.

## Method and caveats

- **The board is the 09:50 state.** The price is the close of the bar stamped 09:45; the volume to 09:50 is the sum of the bars stamped 09:30 to 09:45. The live index quote in Market context is 10:22 ET and is labelled as such; everything else is the 09:50 print.
- **The 09:50 trigger was evaluated on a pre-filtered subset.** The universe was cut before the pull as a union of three tests — turnover at or above $100k, a move or gap of 2pp or more, and no price — leaving 7,197 symbols of a 10,060-name tradeable universe. The names the filter dropped are a disclosed hole, and the trigger was never evaluated on them.
- **`pace` is a screen, not a measurement.** It is measured against the **0.125** opening-share constant, and the ten daily medians behind that constant span **10.77%–16.49%**; a pace between 1.5 and 2.0 on a high-share session is really about 1.2. **`pace` is never comparable with the pre-close slot's `RVol`** — different quantities on different denominators.
- **The pace basis is this session's own same-window ratio** (the 5-minute series and the daily bar fetched back to back), not the archived prior-session factor; 42 names whose own measurement was unusable keep the archived factor and are flagged, and the archived pace is published beside the session's own in the candidates CSV for the pre-close slot to settle.
- **Two data defects were repaired and disclosed, not smoothed over** — the BITO 1-for-2 reverse split (the name dropped out of the published set on the trigger) and the 5-minute volume cap on the names whose opening print exceeded 8x their own median bar volume (one name's pace was republished and one name left the set on the dollar floor).
- **The gap verdict covers twenty minutes, not the session.** `held` means the gap held for twenty minutes. The bell verdicts are the pre-close slot's job.
- **The catalyst research is scoped.** 26 names carry a researched, dated reason; the rest carry the contract's default (`no same-session company catalyst located`) plus the sector driver the name actually traded with. A row with no timestamp still carries its reason — the missing tape costs the timestamp, not the reason.
- **The ntrt gap brief was not published for today** — cron #25 did not leave `ntrt/2026-11-19.md` in the outputs repo, so the pre-market catalysts came from the cron #08 pre-market gapper and from this run's own research. The board does not depend on it.
- **The halt state is not measurable** — the scanner's `halted` column is null on every row, so no halt count is published anywhere on this board.
