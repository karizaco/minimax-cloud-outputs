> **Read this first:** Monday was **one macro event wearing a hundred ticker tags**. Brazil's presidential runoff — decided Sunday night — produced the single largest one-day repricing of a national equity market in this cycle, and it did it in a single session: **Lula won a fourth term with 51.8% of the vote against Flávio Bolsonaro's 48.2%**, becoming the first candidate since 1989 to win a runoff after losing the first round. The **real fell ~11%**, the **Ibovespa fell >15%** (its worst day since 1998, tripping a 30-minute circuit breaker — the first since the 2020 pandemic), and **EWZ closed -19.8%**. Every US-listed Brazilian name broke: **XP -48.4%**, **INTR -34.9%**, **BBD -28.0%**, **PAGS -25.2%**, **ITUB -20.4%**, **NU -17.0%**, **PBR -14.0%**, **SBS -26.1%**. That is one trade, not fifteen ideas, and it is the day's dominant lesson: **position for the country, not the ticker**. The rest of Monday was a risk-on AI day — semis +2.7–2.9%, an Anthropic IPO filing lifting the AI complex, Tesla +5.2% on a Barclays upgrade — with the caveat that the *riskiest* part of the tape (Brazilian financials) was repricing on **fiscal** risk, not on any single company's news.

**The macro driver (Brazil).** Lula's win reversed three weeks of positioning built on Flávio Bolsonaro's surprise first-round lead; the **BRL fell 10.5–11%** to its weakest since December 2025, Brazilian dollar bonds sank across the curve, and JPMorgan downgraded Brazil's currency and rates to market-weight from overweight while Bradesco BBI cut its local-equity outlook to neutral. The market's objection was **fiscal**: a president seen as unlikely to deliver the austerity needed to stabilise debt, into a Congress where Bolsonaro's Liberal Party holds its largest bench in decades, with the finance-minister appointment now the signal to watch. **Bolsonaro refused to concede** and is seeking annulment of the result — so the event risk is not closed. Monday's index gain and Monday's VIX drop are best read as **relief, not conviction** — which the following sessions duly tested.

## Method & caveats

- **Companion file:** `2026-10-26-candidates.csv` — the full priced screen with tier, flags and catalyst, one row per name (319 rows).
- **Universe:** NYSE/NASDAQ/AMEX common stock plus major ETFs. Excluded: warrants, rights, units, preferreds, blank-check SPACs and single-stock/leveraged ETFs. 829 names were priced from daily bars; **319** passed the liquidity floors and the ≥3% move trigger.
- **Liquidity floors applied (midday-scale, to a near-close print):** price ≥ $1; market cap ≥ $50M (soft); session $ volume ≥ ~$2M; move ≥ ~3%.
- **RVol** = session volume ÷ trailing-20-session average volume, computed from the same Yahoo daily bars used for the close. Known bias: a 20-session denominator is more conservative than a 10-session one, so RVol here sits below the TradingView `relative_volume_10d_calc` figures quoted in the Oct 28 digest for the same names.
- **Price-source discipline.** Every close and percentage in this digest is the **Oct 26 regular-session bar from the Yahoo chart API**. During this run the **Nasdaq bulk screener and the Yahoo predefined screener were serving stale sessions** (see below), so neither was used for pricing or filtering; the Nasdaq `/info` endpoint was used only for the Oct 28 pre-market prints in the companion Oct 29 file.
- **Session $ volume** = official close × settled full-day share volume (the regular-session bar), not a mid-session snapshot.
- **Tier rules applied.** Tier A = floors + elevated RVol + material same-session catalyst (direction-agnostic). Soft-A = high-material same-session catalyst with known-soft RVol. TECH_WATCH = floors + soft-trigger move + real turnover, no hard catalyst, RVol ~1–2×. Tier B = floors + move, weak or no catalyst. Tier C = deal-risk/arb only. **RVol was deliberately not a hard gate**, per the US-slot rule.
- **Buyout rule enforced.** SGRY and RRC were held out of the momentum tiers despite 33× and 9.8× RVol and 48% and 18% moves, because both were trading within ~1% of their deal terms.
- **One place the rule was deliberately bent, and flagged:** XTND carries a filing-grade catalyst (8-K item 2.02) and 39.7× RVol, so it sits in Tier C with the microcap flag rather than in the momentum tiers — the catalyst is real, the float is not.
- **Unverified items are labelled, not smoothed over.** ORIC's cause (a J&J data presentation) and AVBP's sell-the-news reversal were established from wire coverage of the specific presentations, not from filings; EAF, AGBK, NAUT and CATX have **no confirmed same-session catalyst** and are in the annex rather than the tiers; MELI's -7.8% (gap -12.8%, $4.02B, RVol 5.07x) is a **Latin-America read-across**, not a Brazil filing, and was classified as Brazil-linked on that basis; the MELI move is deliberately **not** in Tier A because its country attribution is a read rather than a domestic event.
- **The clock incident.** This file was published on **Oct 29** for the **Oct 26** session because the container clock was stale at fire time and the run was suspended before it could publish. Nothing in the data was reconstructed from memory: the closes, volumes, RVols and catalysts were all captured live against the Oct 26 session, and the screen was re-run against the same bars during the Oct 29 execution to confirm every number in this file.
- **MCP availability:** `tool_search` returns **zero** registered `mcp__tradingview_advanced__*` or `mcp__yahoo_finance__*` tools in this sandbox, so the skill's TradingView budget did not apply and no screener/news MCP corroboration was possible. Catalysts are filing-grade (EDGAR 8-K item codes), issuer-grade (Business Wire, company IR) or wire-grade (Reuters, CNBC, Investing.com, Briefing.com, Perplexity Finance aggregation of wire copy).
