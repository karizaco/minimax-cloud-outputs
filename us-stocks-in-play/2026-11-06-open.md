## The headline

**The AI trade bought the dip, and the jobs report paid for it.** Yesterday was the worst session for the Nasdaq since April 2025: the Nasdaq Composite **-5.52%**, the S&P 500 **-4.36%**, the Dow **-3.44%** (its worst post-midterm day since 1896), the PHLX Semiconductor Index **-12.25%** (its worst since March 2020) and roughly **$2.3–3.0trn** of market value, on the fear that a Democratic sweep would block US data-centre permitting. Asia caught up overnight — Kospi **-6%**, Nikkei **-5.96%**, SK hynix **-11.7%**, Taiwan **-8.5%** — and South Korea and Taiwan intervened to prop up their markets. At **09:50 ET** the reversal is already most of the way through: **NVDA +6.86%** on **$17.33bn** of turnover in twenty minutes (pace **5.67**), **AMD +12.98%**, **MU +9.46%**, **SNDK +11.58%**, **TSM +7.43%**, **SKHY +6.88%**, **ASML +6.33%**, and the SOX bouncing about 4%.

The caveat that changes how this should be read: **the 08:30 ET print was hawkish, not friendly.** October payrolls came in at **+165,000** against a **+60–65,000** consensus, unemployment fell to **4.1%** from 4.2%, and September was revised up to +60,000 from +29,000. The **10-year is up ~4bp to 4.88%** and December hike odds are back to **~78%**. That is why the defensives are the other side of the trade — **XLP -1.60%, XLU -0.81%, XLF -0.38%, and the Dow -0.27%** while the Nasdaq-100 is **+2.12%** and the S&P 500 **+0.93%**. This is a **rotation inside a hawkish tape**, not a broad risk-on session, and the VIX is still **24.89** even after a **-11.67%** collapse.

The second caveat: **this is a bounce, not a repair.** The mechanism that caused the rout is a **post, not a bill** — Ansari's day-1 AI-pause language was never legislated. Jefferies calls Thursday "reactionary, not a fundamental reset" and LYNX Equity says *"dip a toe in"*, but AMD is still **-33% over a month** and Intel **-51%** after today's bounce, and the semiconductor index is still ~8% below where it closed before the rout. The names with a **story of their own** are the ones worth trading: **IREN +22.63%** (Q1 FY27, AI-cloud revenue nearly tripled to ~$200m — but the >$4bn contracted-revenue target slips to end-February), **NET +11.46%**, **ABNB +9.78%**, **FROG +30.66%**, **TMDX +18.11%** — and against them **DKNG -27.17%** and **IRTC -26.48%**, where the print *was* the story and the print was bad.

## Method and caveats

- **Every price is the 09:50 ET state, rebuilt from 5-minute bars.** The screen was pulled between 09:51 and 09:58 ET; the `chg @ 09:57 ET` column is the last 5-minute bar available when the run finished, so a reader can see what the 09:50 snapshot missed. The market-context quotes are the bridge's own 09:57 ET prints.
- **The gap verdicts cover 20 minutes, not the session.** `held` = "held for 20 minutes". The pre-close fire at 15:50 ET owns the held-into-the-bell question, and its gap annex is a different screen with a different floor.
- **The pace measure is a screening measure, not a measurement.** The 0.125 opening-share constant is regime-dependent — the skill's own measurement put the daily median between **10.77% and 16.49%** — so a published pace of 2.0 is roughly 1.8–2.3 on a different session's regime. The Yahoo 5-minute series is reconciled to each symbol's own daily bar by a **per-symbol factor measured on the prior session** (median 0.894); the residual risk is that the missing ~11% of volume is not perfectly proportional through the day, so a single name's pace can carry **±20%** of error. **The pace is not comparable with the pre-close slot's RVol** — the column is labelled `pace` everywhere, including in the CSV.
- **Screen completeness:** the trigger legs were pulled atomically and each was verified against its own `totalCount` after a paginated pull of the same universe silently returned duplicate rows for 3,876 symbols. The universe was then pulled in **one unpaginated call per instrument type** (6,093 equities, 4,677 funds) and screened locally, so there is no pagination hole and no top-N cap. **The 09:50 move trigger itself carries one honest hole**: a name that was more than 3% *at 09:50* but was flat by the time the scanner was pulled at ~09:58 ET, whose pre-market gap was under 2%, and whose turnover was under 750k shares, would not have entered the candidate set — it would have to be found by name.
- **Not verified:** the proximate cause of several large moves could not be confirmed with a dated source and they are published with the price, the pace and **no catalyst claim** — among them **SMR +13.62%**, **PENG +15.61%**, **FORM +12.15%**, **MXL +10.60%** and **ONDS +8.96%**. **AKAM +8.43%** is published in TECH_WATCH rather than Tier A because its print could not be confirmed; what *is* confirmed is three brokers cutting targets this morning (RBC $135→$85 at 09:19 ET, Susquehanna $140→$90, Craig-Hallum $190→$170). **TSLA and PLTR are not on the board** — neither cleared the 09:50 trigger.
- **The fuel log's `errors` count is the number of degraded sources, not the number of failed symbols.** The 28 symbols that failed bar reconstruction are dead or unlisted names, and the 599 names that were on yesterday's board and are absent today are a screen result, not a source failure.
- **This is a live board, not a settled one.** Nothing here is a closing price, and the pre-close fire at 15:50 ET publishes the settled version of the same screen with `RVol` and the full-session volume floor.

**degraded_sources: 2** — (1) the TradingView scanner's `halted` column is null on every row, so **the halt trigger could not be evaluated** this session; (2) **28 of the 672 candidates could not be reconstructed from 5-minute bars** (dead or unlisted symbols) and are carried on the scanner's own values, which is why their `price_source` column reads `tv_scanner` and their price is a 09:5x print rather than a 09:50 reconstruction.
