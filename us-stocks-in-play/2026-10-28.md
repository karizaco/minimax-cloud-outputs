**The macro driver of the day.** The FOMC left the target range at **3.75–4.00%** (as expected, ~90% priced), but the vote was **10–2 with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissenting in favour of a 25bp hike**, and the statement upgraded activity from "solid" to **"strong"** while repeating that inflation "remains elevated." That combination is the market's worst kind: no decision to trade, but a clearly higher path. December is now treated as a foregone conclusion (~85–90% on FedWatch) after the September 25bp hike. The starting point was hot September CPI (**3.7% y/y, up from 3.4%; core 2.6% y/y; +0.6% m/m headline**) and September PPI at **6.3% y/y**, the steepest in four years. Rates responded accordingly: 2-year +3.9bp to **4.776%**, 10-year +5bp to **5.047%**, 30-year +3.8bp to **5.338%**. WTI crude added $2.94 to **$102.82**; the dollar was flat near 102.

**Why the indexes are green but the day feels violent.** The FOMC was the 2:00 PM catalyst and it landed into a tape that had already re-rated on two overnight prints. The constructive side was entirely *AI infrastructure and memory*: SK hynix's sevenfold profit surge gave the semiconductor complex its catalyst, and Oracle's Investor Day did the same for data-centre power and cooling. The Fed then lifted the discount rate but could not dent the AI capex narrative. Everything else — software, homebuilders, logistics, consumer-discretionary restaurants, solar — sold its own news. **That is the whole day:** one trade up, the rest of the market down on idiosyncratic disappointment, with volatility *falling* because the vol market was trading the Fed, not the equity dispersion.

## Method & caveats

- **Universe:** NYSE/NASDAQ/AMEX common stock plus major ETFs. Excluded: warrants, rights, units, preferreds, blank-check SPACs, and single-stock/leveraged ETFs. The blanket screens rejected 74 preferreds, 30 blank-check SPACs, 14 warrant/unit tickers, 11 sub-$1 names, 6 sub-$50M microcaps, 214 names below the $2M turnover floor and 289 names with a move under 3%.
- **Discovery coverage is two independent lists, not one.** Yahoo's four predefined mover lists returned **628 unique names** (day_gainers 178, day_losers 153, most_actives 273, small_cap_gainers 110) and the TradingView per-country scanner returned **1,263 rows across four sorts** inside a market-cap ≥$50M and price ≥$1 filter, out of 4,537 qualifying names. Merging both is what caught SKHY and GMAB — two of the day's largest moves — which a single curated list would likely have missed. The residual gap: a ≥5% mover that *both* lists omitted for any window could have been missed, and the 446-name priced set is a floor, not a census.
- **Liquidity floors applied (midday-scale, to a near-close print):** price ≥ $1; market cap ≥ $50M; session $ volume ≥ ~$2M; move ≥ ~3%. 1,603 raw candidates → 507 passed → 446 priced with a settled close.
- **RVol** = session volume ÷ 10-session average volume (TradingView `relative_volume_10d_calc` / `average_volume_10d_calc`). **Known denominator bias:** TradingView's 10-day average volume measures **6–16% tighter than Yahoo's trailing-10-session average** (measured across 12 symbols on 2026-10-28, median ~0.90×), so TV-based RVol is **overstated by roughly that margin** versus a Yahoo denominator. It is stated as the direction of the bias because it could flip a bucket. **SKHY and GMAB have no RVol baseline** (no TradingView average-volume history for either), and both are placed in Tier A under the pipeline rule that a missing RVol is not a disqualifier — but read them as unquantified, not as quiet.
- **Price-source discipline.** Three sources were compared for the closing print and they do not agree perfectly: **Yahoo chart API** (regular-session bar), **TradingView's** post-close scanner settle, and **Nasdaq `/info`**. Nine names diverged by more than 1.2% on price or 1.0pp on percentage change between sources, and **Nasdaq was the one that failed** — its `primaryData` switches to the *after-hours* print within seconds of the close, so a naive fetch after 16:00 returns the AH price with an AH percentage while the field still says "Open". The fix was to read `secondaryData` once `marketStatus` flips to `After-Hours`, and to treat **Yahoo's regular-session bar as authoritative** with TradingView as the cross-check. All quoted closes and percentage changes in this digest are the **official regular-session settle**; after-hours prints are excluded by construction. Settlement lag: closing prints captured 16:01–16:08 ET.
- **Session $ volume** = official close × settled full-day share volume. The first pass used a pre-close (15:52 ET) volume snapshot, which understated the heaviest names by up to ~30% (MU, for example) and would have mis-ranked the top of the book; the column above uses settled volume throughout, with the source recorded per row in the CSV.
- **Tier rules applied.** Tier A = floors + material same-session catalyst (direction-agnostic — a beat-and-raise that sold, like FFIV, qualifies, and so does a miss that was bought, like STLA). Soft-A = high-material same-session catalyst with *known-soft or missing* RVol. TECH_WATCH = floors + soft-trigger move + real turnover, no same-session catalyst, RVol ~0.5–2.0×. Tier B = floors + move, weak or no catalyst. Tier C = deal-risk/arb only. RVol was deliberately **not** a hard gate at any point, per the US-slot rule.
