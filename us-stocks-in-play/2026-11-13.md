## Method and caveats

- **Every published price and volume is the SETTLED 2026-11-13 regular-session close and the settled regular-session volume.** The close, the previous close and the session volume are the bridge's `market-recap` post-bell regular-session print, cross-checked against the Nasdaq official quote API's post-bell `secondaryData` field. The change percentage is recomputed from the **same** source's close and previous close, so no row mixes a close from one source with a percentage from another.
- **One source per field.** The `polygon-io` bar for the session being published had not consolidated upstream at run time, so the settled session volume is the bridge's, not the scanner's — and the scanner's `volume` column is never published as the numerator. The bridge's post-bell volume is **a floor**: the closing auction consolidates after the bell, and the runtime contract measures the same read running 0.7%-30.2% low on other days.
- **RVol = settled session volume ÷ the trailing-10 settled average from the same series** (the 10 sessions ending 2026-11-12, market-wide from `polygon-io grouped_daily`, 11,974 symbols with a complete denominator). The screener's own RVol column uses a different denominator and is **not** mixed in. **RVol is a soft preference at this slot**, and the RVol ≥ 2.0 Tier A bar is what pushes the mega-cap leaders — MRVL 0.58, WDC 0.54, LRCX 0.60, APP 0.38, LITE 0.47 — out of Tier A on a day when they were the biggest names on the board. **That is the finding, not an omission.**
- **The gap verdicts are computed at the bell, not at 15:50.** At the pre-close slot the question is whether a gap *held into the bell*, so the annex verdicts compare the session's own opening gap against the **settled 16:00 close** of the same session. The bands are the shared helper's: `|gap| < 2%` = `no_move`, a sign flip = `reverse`, the move given back = `faded`, otherwise `held`. **The intermediate path points (10:00 / 12:00 / 15:50) are not published: Yahoo's chart endpoint answered HTTP 429 for the 5-minute series after the session's own daily pulls, so the intraday path could not be rebuilt this run.** That is a disclosed loss of detail, not a change in the measure — the open and the bell are the two points the verdict uses, and the bell here is the bridge's own settled close rather than a 5-minute reconstruction, which is the stronger of the two.
- **The pre-close slot ARBITRATES the open slot's pace basis.** The 09:50 board publishes two pace bases for every name — `pace` on the archived-prior-session factor and `pace_live_basis` on the live feed — and this board's settled session volume is the ground truth. **The reading, against the measured band: the live basis is the right one, and the published `pace` column overstates.** On the names whose opening pace HELD into the bell, the settled RVol sits far closer to `pace_live_basis` than to `pace` — CBRS 3.20 settled against 8.02 live and 13.24 published, MOD 2.68 against 6.14 and 7.09, NU 2.11 against 2.30 and 2.42, SLSN 85.15 against 74.91 and 12,767.70, MRLN 15.51 against 53.87 and 600.61. On the names whose opening pace FADED through the session, **both** pace bases overstate the settled RVol — AMD 1.01 settled against 1.35 live and 1.39 published, CSCO 1.12 against 1.77 and 2.65, SNDK 0.78 against 1.57 and 1.70, NBIS 1.57 against 3.03 and 3.18. **Two separate biases are therefore visible: the ~12% factor bias the archived-prior-session denominator adds, and a regime bias the pace measure carries when the session fades — it extrapolates the 09:50 pace across a session that did not keep it.** The 10.77%-16.49% opening-share band explains a ±15% swing in a pace reading; it does **not** explain a 25% gap between the live basis and the settled RVol on the faded names (AMD 1.35 live against a settled 1.01), because that gap is the session's own fade and not the band. **The published `pace` column is not changed in this commit — the basis change lands separately.**

- **The pace and the RVol are never comparable.** `pace` is a partial-session measure on an elapsed-session denominator (the 0.125 opening-share constant, whose ten daily medians span 10.77%-16.49%); `RVol` is a full-session measure on a trailing-10 full-session denominator. They are different quantities on different denominators, and the pre-close board labels its column `RVol` and never `pace`. **The pace column is not changed in the same commit that publishes this comparison.**
- **The board is wide by construction and the tradeable end is thin.** 594 names cleared the floors, of which **20 are Tier A, 8 Soft-A and 3 Tier C**. The 192 TECH_WATCH and 371 Tier B names are the *beta*, not stories — they are published so the imbalance is visible, not because they are tradeable. **The counts in the tier headings are the full tier counts; the tables are capped.**
- **Every row carries its catalyst provenance.** `has_catalyst_timestamp` is `yes` where the catalyst carried a verified timestamp — an 8-K, a dated release, a dated wire — and `no` otherwise, and `reason_without_timestamp` carries the **reason** on every one of the rows — including the explicit "no same-session company catalyst located" default on names where a search was run and found nothing, so a re-tier can never wipe the reasons that were researched.
- **Where a print landed but its figures were not sourced, the row says exactly that.** Stantec, Newsmax's predecessors, GCT Semiconductor, Merlin, Cineverse, SBC Medical and MRLN-type rows carry the release and the tape, not numbers nobody checked.
- **The tier tables are rendered from the same dataset the CSV is written from**, so a number cannot be right in one file and wrong in the other.
- **This board is a reconstruction of a settled session, not a live board.** Every number is settled; the post-market brief owns the after-close earnings reactions, and this digest's `overnight_carry` hands the watch list forward rather than re-telling it.
