## The headline

**The political AI de-rate became a rout, and this time it did not stay a rotation — it took the whole market with it.** Yesterday the blue wave priced a *trade*: money out of AI hardware, money into AI spenders, the Dow up 3.03% and the Nasdaq-100 up 0.91%. Today the same mechanism ran in reverse and kept running. The **Philadelphia Semiconductor Index closed -12.25%**, **SMH -11.21%**, **SOXQ -12.13%**, **QQQ -6.49%**, the **Nasdaq-100 -6.36%**, the **Nasdaq Composite -5.52%**, the **S&P 500 -4.36%** and the **Dow -3.44% (-1,833 pts)**. **Every one of the eleven S&P 500 sectors closed red** — the rotation of Wednesday had nowhere left to rotate into. The **VIX closed 27.14, +63.4%** from 16.61, and the **VXX +30.13%**.

The mechanism is the same one the 09:50 board flagged, but the tape stopped believing the earnings. Rep. Yassamin Ansari's post on the night of 11-04 — *"Top of the Democratic agenda on day 1 needs to be a pause on advanced AI development, regulation of this out of control industry, and a ban on artificial superintelligence"* — became a **build-out-kill switch** once the state governorships in Texas, Ohio and Georgia put permitting and power costs on the table. **UBS put about 30% of the 2028 data-centre additions at risk** (Reuters). A report that **Microsoft is delaying data-centre spending** hit IREN specifically, and the Texas-data-centre complex took its own leg down (**Applied Digital -17%, Cipher -21%, TeraWulf -13%**). And a *'they're shooting the generals'* framing circulated on the tape — hedge funds de-risking a crowded 2026 AI position rather than re-underwriting it.

**The dollar volume is the tell.** **NVDA -8.91% on $54.8bn** — the largest dollar volume on the board and, on its own, the heaviest weight on the S&P 500 — then **MU -17.77% on $49.3bn**, **AMD -17.39% on $28.1bn**, **SNDK -22.42% on $23.6bn**, **TSM -11.16% on $14.7bn**, **AVGO -10.15% on $15.4bn**, **MRVL -16.63% on $8.7bn**, **LITE -18.93% on $5.0bn**. The memory complex led: **SNDK -22.42%, MU -17.77%, WDC -17.56%, STX -16.66%, SKHY -12.85%** — names whose record quarters were built on exactly the spending cycle now in question.

**The caveat that changes how this should be read: this was not an earnings verdict, and it was not confined to the AI complex.** AMD had just reported the **largest and most profitable quarter in its history** — revenue **$13.38bn, +44.7% y/y**, ahead of the $13.08bn consensus — and Lisa Su raised the long-term guide on the call to *"more than 80%"* revenue growth in 2027. TSM guided to a **record $53.3bn revenue midpoint**. Coherent signed a **$20bn hyperscaler deal**. Arista guided a strong networking outlook. **Every one of them was sold.** That tells you the de-rate is a *multiple* event on the spending cycle, not a verdict on the companies, and it is as reversible as Wednesday's was. The second caveat: **the defensives did not catch it.** Yesterday's bid names gave it back — **XLP +3.76% → -0.75%, XLV +3.23% → -2.50%, XLF +3.38% → -2.85%** — and the banks broke with the market (**GS -6.47% on RVol 2.01, JPM -4.17%**). Only **bonds, gold, oil and China held**: **LQD +0.15%, TLT +0.21%, GLD +0.81%, USO +1.93%, GDX +1.37%, KWEB +1.81%**. Even crypto did not: **bitcoin -5.65% to below $76,000**, with **MSTR -8.66% and COIN -9.23%**. That is a **broad de-risking**, not a rotation, and it is why the VIX moved 63% rather than 20%.

**The one bright spot was a print, and it was a company print.** **APP +31.01%** on a Q3 beat with a raised outlook and cash generation backing a **larger buyback**, with the broker note flow already stacked behind it pre-open (**Jefferies $430, Scotiabank $450, BTIG $450**) — **$7.76bn of turnover, RVol 4.40**. Also **U +13.99%** (Q3 beat, robust Q4 outlook, RVol 4.54), **EPAM +21.25%** (Q3 beat and a 2026 guidance raise), **TPR +7.55%** (fiscal Q1 beat and a better FY27 outlook). Those four are the only large-cap long-side events on the board, and all four are company news rather than theme.

## Method and caveats

- **Prices are the official 2026-11-05 regular-session close** from Nasdaq `/info` `secondaryData`, stamped *"Closed at Nov 5, 2026 4:00 PM ET"*. **Volumes are the settled session volume** from the Yahoo daily bar, which matched shibui's settled RTH volume **exactly (ratio 1.000)** on the 11-04 session for 9 of 11 sampled names.
- **The TradingView scanner is ~15 minutes delayed and its post-bell values are after-hours contaminated.** This was measured directly: the scanner's 16:01:30 ET pull showed **MU at 669.33 (-15.83%)**, while the official close was **653.90 (-17.77%)** — the scanner was still showing the ~15:45 print and missed the closing auction. A post-bell re-pull at 16:25 ET drifts again (NVDA 182.03 → 182.94). **The scanner is therefore used here for discovery, the gap columns and the universe only.** The earlier 09:50 board was a reconstruction from 5-minute bars for the same reason.
- **RVol is a floor on 194 of the 1,933 tiered names** whose Yahoo daily bar had not yet ingested through 16:00 ET at the time of the sweep; their session volume is a partial and their RVol is understated. The published RVol is the settled volume ÷ the trailing-10 settled average, both from the same series — **a screener's own RVol column uses a different denominator and is not comparable**, and it is never mixed into a published row.
- **Screen completeness:** the trigger screen is `|chg| ≥ 3% OR |gap| ≥ 3%` on the **settled** move over 3,114 priced US equities. Every survivor of the floors was priced (1,933 tiered). There is no unpriced tail and no top-N cap. The universe is the **union of the 15:52 ET and 16:25 ET scanner pulls** so that the scanner's post-bell re-labelling of the session cannot drop a name that qualified earlier; the residual risk is a name that moved ≥ 3% only in the final eight minutes of regular trading and qualified in neither pull.
