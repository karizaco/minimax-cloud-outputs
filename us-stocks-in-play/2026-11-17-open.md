## The headline

**The morning opened flat and sold off all day, and this board is the 09:50 picture of a market that had not yet found its story.** At 09:50 the index was a rounding error: SPY -0.15%, QQQ -0.07%, IWM -0.12%, DIA -0.44% (the 09:50 prints, rebuilt from 5-minute bars), and by the bell those were -1.01%, -0.73%, -1.43% and -1.62%. **The board's own shape is therefore not a market selloff — it is two sector stories and a thin, idiosyncratic edge.**

**The sector stories are dated, and they are the board's real content.** Enterprise software was sold on a run of outages — an outage affecting internal NSA communications was reported on Tuesday, on top of Friday's aviation-comms failures, Microsoft's service disruptions and outages at major US banks — and investors priced the risk that software vendors are particularly vulnerable to AI misuse: **Salesforce -5.62% on $228.9m**, ServiceNow -3.71% on $216.2m, Workday -5.44% on $42.1m, Intuit -4.14% on $59.8m, Adobe -3.38%, MongoDB -4.45%, SAP -4.14%. ## Method and caveats

**The pace measure is a screen, not a measurement.** `pace` = (session volume to 09:50 ÷ trailing-10 full-session average volume) ÷ **0.125**, the measured opening share of a normal session. The ten daily medians behind that constant span **10.77%–16.49%**, so a pace of 1.9 is really 1.8–2.3 on another session's regime; treat it as a screen. **The pace column and the pre-close slot's RVol are different quantities on different denominators and are never comparable** — this board publishes `pace` and no RVol column of its own; the one settled RVol it publishes is the arbitration table above, which is there to settle the basis, not to be read as a pace. A pace between 1.5 and 2.0 on a high-share session is a name whose pace is really ~1.2, and it is Soft-A rather than Tier A.

**The published numbers are the 09:50 state, rebuilt from 5-minute bars.** The prior close is the prior session's last non-null bar close, re-based onto Yahoo's own official daily close for 2026-11-16 (218 names re-based, 8 of them dropped from the published set as a result). The volume-to-09:50 numerator is the sum of the bars stamped 09:30–09:45, divided by a per-symbol volume-basis factor measured on the prior session (Yahoo's 5-minute total ÷ polygon's daily volume) so the numerator and the denominator sit on one basis; the factor's source is published per row in the CSV. The reconstructed 09:50 move for the index was within **0.02pp** of an independent Nasdaq 1-minute read.

**The volume-basis factor, and the repair.** The published `pace` divides today's 09:50 numerator (the volume in the 09:30–09:45 bars) by a factor measured on the PRIOR session: Yahoo's 5-minute total for 2026-11-16 over polygon's daily volume for 2026-11-16. That factor's job is to put today's numerator on the daily basis, and it is a proxy because at 09:50 the day's own daily bar is not final. **For 46 of the 266 published names Yahoo's archived 2026-11-16 5-minute series is inconsistent with its own daily bar, and the proxy then fails by more than the opening-share regime noise the pace measure already carries (the 0.125 constant's own daily medians span 10.77%–16.49%). Those factors are replaced by the symbol's own current-session 5-minute-to-daily ratio — measured on the settled session — and the names are flagged per row in the CSV. Unrepaired, the worst case published a pace of 1,839 on $370.7m for Safe Pro Group where the session-correct figures are 134 on $27.0m.** The floors and the trigger were re-applied after the repair.

**The floors and the trigger.** Price ≥ $1, market cap ≥ $50m (soft), session $ volume to 09:50 ≥ $500,000, and at least one trigger: |move| ≥ 3% or |gap| ≥ 3%. Every survivor of the floors was priced — the board is not capped at the top N by turnover, and the priced count equals the tiered count (266 names).

**What this board is not.** The halt state is not measurable this session, so no halt count is published. Nothing here is a settled close — the settled numbers appear only in the pace-basis arbitration and in Market context, and they are labelled as such. The 15:50 ET fire is a different screen with a different volume floor and a different measure, and it lives in `us-stocks-in-play/2026-11-17.md`. The prior board's figures were not re-verified — where Friday's print is still the driver it is cited from `us-stocks-in-play/2026-11-16-open.md` rather than re-researched.

**The honest gap in this one.** The 09:50 window closed about 11.5 hours before this board was written, and the market kept moving: the index was flat at 09:50 (SPY -0.15%, QQQ -0.07%) and closed -1.01% and -0.73%, with software leading the damage and the VIX at 25.46. **A gap that held for twenty minutes did not necessarily hold into the bell, and this board cannot tell you which ones did** — that is the pre-close board's job, and it published it.
