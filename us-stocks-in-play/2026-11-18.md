## The headline

**The hawkish Fed broke the metals and the managed-care complex broke on a retroactive audit rule, and the tradeable end of the day was a cluster of single-name prints on both sides.** The S&P 500 settled at **7,323.54, -0.23%**, the Nasdaq Composite was **+0.05%**, the Dow **-1.12%** and the Russell 2000 **+0.39%** — but that index print is a small part of the session. **Two factors did the work.** First, **Fed Governor Christopher Waller said the central bank needs to get more aggressive on inflation and either approve a half-point hike in December or communicate a faster pace of increases in 2027**, with Williams and Musalem also signalling more hikes; CME FedWatch prices a 92% chance of a 25bp December hike, the dollar index hit a **19-month high**, and **gold sank to a more-than-one-year low (spot $3,739.39 at 13:47 ET, -2.9%)** — **GLD -3.20%**, **SLV -4.60%**, **GDX -5.97%**, **GDXJ -6.01%**, and the copper complex went with it (**FCX -6.96%**). Second, **CMS proposed the CRUSH rule**, which would run Medicare Advantage audits with no fee-for-service adjuster and **extrapolate findings back to payment year 2018** — about **$9bn of recoveries per payment year** and nearly **$90bn of federal savings over 2027-2036** — and the managed-care group was sold hard: **UnitedHealth -10.21% on $6.0bn (a 3.08x RVol)**, **Humana -12.98%**, **CVS -5.20%**. **WTI fell 3.11% to $82.02**, so energy was sold too, and bitcoin fell 3.61% to $62,531.

**The tradeable end is the idiosyncratic print, and on this tape it is mostly a distress print.** **Hyster-Yale -41.07%** on a **$125m convertible notes offering** (a 23.0x RVol), **Jumia -40.82%** on a guidance cut that buried a revenue beat, **SkyAI +90.32%** on a tender offer that left about 3.6m shares outstanding, **Prenetics -36.62%** after it roughly tripled its second-half loss guidance, **Tronox -22.40%** on a Wall Street Journal restructuring report it publicly denied, **Ardent Health -18.87%** on an upsized secondary, **Quanterix -27.62%** on a widened loss, and **BellRing Brands +24.07%** on a Q4 beat with the long-term targets pulled. **The one clean momentum print on the board is Take-Two: +6.22% on GTA VI, the gap held into the bell at +4.52%, on 2.29x volume.**

**The caveat that changes how this board should be read: the volume ratio says the tape was sold by macro, not hunted, and the mega-caps are carrying the story.** **AMD +5.31% on $17.6bn**, **Amazon +3.82% on $10.8bn** and **Broadcom +3.64% on $9.1bn** all traded on **less than 0.8x** their own ten-session average — the rotation out of Nvidia into the names that fill the leadership vacuum, with no volume behind any of it. **Nvidia itself fell -2.89% despite a beat-and-raise** (adjusted EPS $2.68 vs $2.48, revenue $115.84bn vs $109.10bn, fiscal Q4 guided to about $130bn, fiscal-2028 growth raised to 75% from 70%, a $150bn buyback), because Kress confirmed no China data-centre revenue is assumed in the outlook, H200 shipments to China have stopped, the board ruled out Huang's return and US agencies are investigating chip diversion into China. **The honest finding is that the macro factor did the selling, the idiosyncratic prints are the tradeable end, and the AI leadership overhang — not the AI numbers — is what the AI complex is actually trading.**

## Method and caveats

- **Every number on this board is rendered from ONE dataset** (`2026-11-18-candidates.csv`, 945 rows), so the board and the CSV cannot disagree. The tables are generated from it, not hand-typed.
- **The prices are settled and the volumes are settled — after a measured correction.** The first post-bell read understated the session volume by a median 2.93% and by up to 2.08x on individual names; the published numbers are the re-read, taken after two consecutive whole-set pulls agreed, and cross-checked against the Nasdaq official tape at a median ratio of 1.00000. **A reader comparing this board with any earlier board published on the same day must use the settled figures, not the first post-bell print.**
- **RVol is a soft preference at this slot, not a hard gate.** The Tier A bar is RVol ≥ 2.0; every other tier is reachable on RVol below 1. The denominator is the trailing 10 settled sessions ending 2026-11-17, computed market-wide from `polygon-io grouped_daily` — the same series and the same map the previous board used, so the RVol column is comparable with the boards before it.
- **The gap verdicts are computed at the BELL (16:00), not at 15:50.** A 15:50 read would have published two of the day's largest gaps wrong: the open board's own gap annex scores the open-to-09:50 window, and this board scores open-to-bell on the settled close.
- **A gap that held into the bell is the signal at this slot; a gap that faded is not.** `held` means the opening move was still largely intact at 16:00; `reverse` means the opening move flipped sign; `faded` means it was given back; `no_move` means the open was inside the 2% noise band and there is no gap to read.
- **A Tier A bar is volume behind a company event, not a good outcome.** Ten of the 14 Tier A names are prints that went the wrong way. A near-offer or a media-reported deal is never promoted to Tier A, and a capital-structure print is Tier C — a watch, not a chase.
- **A sector or macro event is a soft catalyst and never a Tier A company catalyst.** That is why the mega-caps sit in Soft-A at RVol below 1 and not in Tier A.
- **`no same-session company catalyst located` is the honest default on a row where a search was run and found nothing** — it is not a claim that nothing happened, and it is set as the default in the tiering step so a re-tier cannot wipe a researched reason.
- **The open board's pace column and this board's RVol column are never comparable** — different quantities on different denominators. The arbitration table above carries the open board's two pace bases beside the settled RVol; the settled number settles the volume, and the pace stays on the open board as a screen against the 0.125 constant, whose regime band spans 10.77%–16.49%.
- **Degraded sources: 2** — (1) `polygon-io grouped_daily` for the session being published returned HTTP 403 `plan_required`, so the settled session volume came from Yahoo's own post-bell daily bar cross-checked against the Nasdaq official tape instead; (2) `preopen_feed.py` ran but its verdict column is scored against a 15:50 read, so the gap verdicts here are recomputed at the bell from the same 5-minute series. Finviz is egress-blocked and was not needed for any published number, so it is not counted; the scanner's `halted` column is null and the halt state is **not measurable**, which is a property of the data rather than a source failure.
- **The halt state is not measurable this session** — the scanner's `halted` column is null on every row — so no halt count is published anywhere in this digest.
- **Source hygiene:** the Yahoo daily and 5-minute endpoints answered only with a browser User-Agent; the plain request is throttled from this egress. The TradingView scanner pull was one unpaginated call with `totalCount` and unique-symbol counts both asserted at 20,367, and the page union checked for disjointness.
