## 5. Recap — dominant themes of the week

1. **The AI audit opened on politics, not on numbers.** Democratic control of both chambers plus wins in data-centre-heavy states (Texas ~30% of 2028 US data-centre capacity per SemiAnalysis) triggered proposals to slow or stop new data-centre construction — a nationwide moratorium bill from Senators Sanders and Ocasio-Cortez is on the table, 40+ supportive data-centre ballot measures were rejected on Nov 3, and Goldman Sachs is penciling in construction delays in Georgia and Texas. Thursday's indiscriminate selling was the market repricing *capex*, not earnings: all 11 S&P sectors closed lower, worst in AI and tech (Reuters; ATB; Weekly Investing Preview).
2. **Oil broke on diplomacy.** WTI fell ~$10 to $90 on signs of US–Iran talks, with a Democratic Congress creating leverage to end the conflict; energy was the worst sector at −12.0%, and the Nasdaq Chartstopper logged a weekly oil decline of ~$10. The war-funding fight now threatens a year-end government shutdown (CNBC).
3. **A blowout labour report re-armed the hawkish case.** October payrolls +165k (~2.5× the 65k consensus), unemployment down to 4.1%, average hourly earnings +0.4% m/m / +3.1% y/y. CME FedWatch put a December 8–9 hike at **76.8%** on Friday, up from 66.9% a week earlier; Fed funds futures moved to ~80% intraday. Daly: "we're going to have to tighten policy further"; Hammack: policy "will need to move to a restrictive stance"; Barkin: "the policy direction we need to take from here is quite clear"; Williams: inflation "has been too high for too long"; Logan and Musalem also leaned hawkish. Chair Kevin Warsh has declined to guide.
4. **Defensive rotation, and it is already crowded.** Retail trade (+6.2%), consumer services (+3.9%), real estate (+1.9%), staples (+0.7%) and financials (+1.2%) absorbed the flow; CNBC reports retail investors piling into healthcare and staples to the point that "even the safety trades look overbought", and Vanda counted Thursday as the biggest day of retail selling since the Covid pandemic.
5. **A "quiet" risk-off tape.** VIX 19.42 → 25.64 (+32%), spiking to 28.18 on Thursday (+70% in a session). Goldman measured Thursday as the largest single-day market-value loss since April 2025; AP noted a worldwide equity tumble that Friday's session only partly halted.
