## 5. Recap — dominant themes of the week

1. **A relief week, not a conviction week.** The S&P 500's best week since August 3 and its first up week in five came after a 4-week slide that ended with the market pricing a hard landing for the AI trade. The recovery was led by the oil-sensitive and rate-sensitive edges — energy, industrials, staples, healthcare — while the two-day Nvidia unwind capped the index. As CNBC's "3 big things" put it, the AI trade faces "big tests next week."
2. **Nvidia's leadership vacuum became the week's defining event.** Huang resigned Thursday with immediate effect after admitting an affair with a woman US intelligence officials consider a suspected Chinese asset; reports of a second affair with a direct report followed. Kress (interim CEO) and Jones (first-ever chair) inherited the company two days before the most-watched earnings call in the AI trade, with shares ~31% below the October 6 record and several downgrades. The read-across — that the CEO who *is* the AI narrative can be removed by scandal — is what took the chip complex down with it: AVGO −4.8% and AMD −8.8% on Friday, after both had surged earlier in the week.
3. **The inflation fight re-armed itself.** October CPI **+0.4%** (cons +0.3%) and core **+0.3%** (cons +0.2%) — the third consecutive 0.3%+ core print, and BLS published **no year-over-year figure** because the 2025 shutdown left no October 2025 base (the NYT computed roughly 4% headline and 2.8% core). PPI was cooler on the month (**+0.3%** vs +0.4% expected) but the y/y rate hit **6.5%, the highest since November 2022** (Yardeni, MacroFinance). Waller, speaking in London on the morning of the CPI release, said he leaned toward a quarter-point hike and that a half-point "should be considered."
4. **Energy became the hedge against the oil headline.** Monday's crude collapse (WTI −3.5%, Brent −4.6%) on Qatar-brokered Iran talks and Trump's "unlikely to order new strikes" post did **not** take XLE down — the ETF rose. By Thursday, Trump's "we don't need a deal with Iran" pushed Brent +3.3% to $95.82, and **37 vessels were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz in October — the highest monthly total since the war began — with ten more in November**. The equity market is pricing producers on a *normalised* oil price near the $73 pre-war level, so the war premium lands in the equities more slowly than in the crude. XLE was up ~47% year-to-date by Thursday.
5. **A quiet risk-off unwind with a hot bond market underneath.** VIX fell 24.7% to 19.30 and never spiked above 19.92 — yet the 10-year rose 29.5bp to 5.15% and the 2-year rose for a fifth straight session, its largest weekly increase since October 2024. Gold fell 4.6%, TLT 3.1%, bitcoin 9.9% to a three-month low. This was not a growth-scare tape; it was a **discount-rate** tape, and the equity index rose anyway.

## 10. Forward themes — what to watch

1. **Nvidia's first print without its founder is the week's largest single risk.** Consensus ~$109bn revenue (+~91% y/y) and ~$2.47 EPS against its own guide of **$108bn ±2%** at a **74% gross margin** (one point below Q2), set on 26 August — *before* the leadership change and *excluding* China data-centre compute revenue. The number that matters is not the print; it is whether Kress guides the company out of the hole Huang left, and whether the **$279bn of supply commitments** and the single customer at **16% of revenue** still stand. A market that has watched NVDA fall ~31% from its October 6 high will punish a soft guide harder than a soft print (CNBC, IG, FourWeekMBA).
2. **Anthropic's IPO is the AI appetite thermometer, not an AI stock.** Pricing Wednesday night, trading Thursday under **ANT** at **$70–78** for **825m shares** — up to **$64.4bn** raised plus a **$26bn private placement**, valuing the company at up to about **$1.4trn** (CNBC, Yahoo Finance). The deal is reported roughly **six times oversubscribed** and **Nvidia is investing up to $10bn** as an anchor. A strong debut re-rates the whole complex; a poor one, in the same week as Nvidia's print, tells you the AI bid is a positioning trade, not a fundamentals trade.
3. **The consumer check, with a rate-hike backdrop.** Retail sales Tuesday (consensus +0.2% after +0.8%) lands the same morning as Home Depot, and the next three days bring Target, Lowe's, TJX, Walmart, Ross, Gap and BJ's — **all of them within two weeks of Thanksgiving, all of them into a 5.15% 10-year and a hot-CPI backdrop.** HD and LOW are the direct read on housing turnover; WMT is the read on big-ticket volume; TGT and TJX on apparel and inventory discipline. The September control group held at +0.1%; a sub-zero print here is the first crack in the consumer.
4. **Watch the bond market, not the index.** With the Fed's December move priced at 89% and a 10% chance of 50bp, the equity market's support depends on the 10-year staying under ~5.25%. The minutes Wednesday will tell you how hawkish the committee really is; if the dissents broaden beyond Cook and Hammack, the December path reprices and the AI-multiple names have no cushion (the S&P was still 3.8% below its October 6 peak on November 10).
5. **Oil is a live macro variable again.** Ten vessel attacks in the Strait of Hormuz in November, a US crude build of 9 million barrels, and Trump's public rejection of an Iran deal keep the war premium in both the crude and the energy equities. A Hormuz escalation is an **inflation** event first and an equity event second — which is exactly the sequence that broke last week.
