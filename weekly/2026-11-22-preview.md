## TL;DR

- **The AI trade broke the market, and it broke it despite two good news events.** The S&P 500 **−8.61%** (7,561.67 → 6,910.39), its worst week since April 2025; Nasdaq Composite **−10.02%** and into negative territory for 2026; Dow **−8.02%**; Russell 2000 **−7.48%**. Friday alone was **−3.70%** on the S&P, the **seventh straight down day** — the longest streak since 2020 — and both the S&P and the Dow closed in **correction** (more than 10% below their Oct 6 records). The Nasdaq-100 touched **bear-market territory intraday**.
- **Nvidia beat, guided up, and fell 22.92%.** Q3 FY2027 revenue **$115.84bn** vs $109.10bn expected, EPS **$2.68** vs ~$2.47, gross margin **74.9%**, data centre **$107.6bn**; Q4 guide **$130bn ±3%** and FY2028 growth raised to **75%** (from 70%), with supply commitments up to **$390bn** from $279bn — all **assuming zero China data-centre compute**. The stock still closed the week at **$125.53, −22.92%**, its longest weekly losing streak since 2012, after **Reuters reported the SEC is probing Nvidia's accounting** (−14.01% Friday alone).
- **Anthropic's record IPO did not rescue the trade.** **ANT** priced at **$83** (980m shares, **$81.3bn** raised — the largest IPO in history), opened at **$111.50** and closed its debut at **$97.17 (+17.07%)**, a fully diluted **$1.76trn** — then fell **8.5%** on Friday as the tape collapsed. The signal is not "AI demand is fine"; it is that **the AI trade is now a funding question**, and a record-sized IPO competes for the same risk capital.
- **The Fed made it worse, then the bond market bid anyway.** Wednesday's FOMC minutes said **"almost all participants"** judged further hikes likely, put AI demand at the core of the committee's inflation discussion, and noted some members wanted a quarter-point hike in October — with a couple flagging a possible **larger** December move. Waller then floated a **50bp** "shock". Yet the 10-year yield **fell 24bp to 4.912%**, TLT rose **2.6%**, and the VIX closed at **35.89 (+86%)**. Rate relief did not lift equities; the risk premium did the selling.
- **Next week is short, heavy, and binary.** Markets are closed Thursday (Thanksgiving) and close early Friday. Wednesday **08:30 ET** stacks **October core PCE (3.2% y/y est vs 3.1%)**, the **Q3 GDP second estimate (3.4–3.5% est)** and **durable goods** — the last full inflation gauge before the Dec 8–9 FOMC, where a **25bp hike is priced at ~96–97%**. **Dell** (Tue AMC), **Analog Devices** (Tue BMO) and **Deere** (Wed BMO) are the AI-demand and consumer reads.

## 5. Recap — dominant themes of the week

1. **The AI trade stopped being a growth story and became a funding story.** Nvidia guided **$130bn** for the quarter, raised FY2028 growth to 75%, and lifted supply commitments to **$390bn**, while raising a **$81.3bn** IPO competed for the same risk capital. Baird's Michael Antonelli put it plainly: *"The whole notion that AI can continue to spend unlimited amounts of money and support the stock market … that's what's being priced right now."* The market did not sell the numbers; it sold the **financing** of the numbers.
2. **Nvidia's leadership vacuum turned into a legal overhang.** CEO **Jensen Huang resigned** the week before (effective immediately), with **Colette Kress** interim CEO and **Harvey Jones** as first-ever chair; Kress's print was strong and she refused to discuss Huang's reinstatement. Then, on Friday, **Reuters reported the SEC is probing Nvidia's accounting** — with the focus on potential accounting issues, and with last year's **geographic-revenue reporting change** (customer headquarters instead of billing addresses, which removed Singapore from the geographic breakdown while Singapore sales approached half of revenue) back in focus. The stock fell 14.01% on the report and **47.5% below its Oct 6 record close** by week's end.
3. **The Fed delivered the hawkishness the market had been dreading — and the market sold anyway.** The **Oct 27–28 minutes** (released Wed 14:00 ET) said *"almost all participants"* judged further hikes likely, described AI demand as **"a material source of inflation risk"**, called inflation pressures *"broad based"*, recorded that some members wanted a quarter-point hike in October, and noted a couple saw the possibility of a **larger increase** in December. Waller then said a quarter-point without a clear signal of a January follow-up would not work, and that the Fed should **"shock people" with 50bp**. CME FedWatch as of Nov 20 priced the Dec 9 move at **~96–97% for 25bp** and **~3% for 50bp** (Reuters, Trading Economics and Trading Economics' Thursday split of ~90/10 differ by a few points).
4. **A record IPO was the wrong reassurance.** Anthropic's debut was a triumph of mechanics — the largest IPO in history, a $2trn intraday valuation, a $1.76trn close — and it **lost 8.5% on Friday** as the market sold everything AI. A record deal printing well and then being sold is the clearest evidence yet that the marginal buyer of AI risk had gone away.
5. **The de-risking was indiscriminate inside risk, and surgical inside quality.** Gold −9.5%, silver −16.2%, bitcoin −13.9%, copper and the Nasdaq-100 in a bear-market intraday touch, while staples, utilities, healthcare and retailers absorbed the flow — Walmart, Costco, CVS and McDonald's were all green on Friday. The long end rallied 24bp. That is a **liquidity event inside the risk complex**, not a macro event: the bond market was not the seller this week.
