## Why a tape was not published even though the capture itself succeeded

The capture ran and returned 379 records, but its PRICE layer does not measure a pre-market
move, so it was rejected rather than published. Measured on this fire:

- **The scanner's pre-market reference close is STALE by one session.** For **242 of 242**
  tickers common to the 2026-11-25 and 2026-11-26 tapes, the implied reference close
  (price ÷ (1 + pct)) is IDENTICAL on both days. The 2026-11-26 band was therefore measured
  against the **2026-11-24** close, while the last completed regular session (2026-11-25)
  closed elsewhere — the scanner never rolls the pre-market anchor when the market is shut.
  The index shows it directly: **QQQ** `close` 633.93 (the 2026-11-25 session, `change`
  −0.65%) against a pre-market print of 617.20 at −3.28%, which implies a reference of
  **638.08** — not the 633.93 it actually closed at.
- **The published moves are therefore wrong, sometimes badly.** NRSN was published at
  **+93.64%**; against its actual last close of 1.76 the print of 2.13 is **+21.02%** —
  inflated 4.5×. RAYA was published at +85.93% against a true +20.61%. **ABCL's sign was
  flipped**: published at −3.11%, actually +0.19% (10.61 vs a 10.59 close). ACHR was
  published at −4.51% against a true −9.54%.
- **The band is an artifact of that stale base, not a market.** It came back **168 up /
  951 down** (85% decliners) — a market that closed lower on 2026-11-25 leaves the stale
  reference sitting ABOVE most names, so nearly everything reads as a decliner. The
  procedure's documented reference band was 271 up / 187 down (2026-11-13).

The catalyst layer the capture produced was real and dated (SEC EDGAR filings, one article,
eight router answers), but a tape whose `pct` column — the one thing it exists to carry — is
anchored to the wrong close is the same defect the procedure records for 2026-11-09
("publishing Friday's move as if it was Monday's pre-market"), and it must not be published.
