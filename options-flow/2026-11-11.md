## Caveats

1. **The own-history baseline is a 4-venue number and it lags the live session.** Cboe's historical service covers only the Cboe-operated venues (CBOE, BATS, C2, EDGX) while the chain covers every US venue, so the *ratio* is internally consistent (4-venue vs 4-venue) but the absolute volumes are a different universe from the chain column beside them. The service also publishes the current session with a lag, so the baseline here is measured on 2026/11/10 while the contract screen is 2026-11-11 — one session apart, labelled on every row.
2. **The peer median is over strikes with volume >= 50.** Including the long tail of near-dead strikes would inflate every peer ratio; excluding them would suppress it. The group median and group size are published on every row so the ratio is auditable.
3. **A ratio on a trivial number is still a trivial number.** A high own-history ratio can rest on a few hundred contracts — always read the ratio next to the volume.
4. **The composite is an ensemble, not a statistic.** Every component is published per row so the ranking can be re-derived or re-weighted; a single-metric view is a sort away.
5. **V/OI is an upper bound on a gap day.** Cboe's open interest is the prior session's settled OI while the volume is today's session, so V/OI is inflated by construction when fresh positions open all session. The OI >= 50 slice in the series book exists for this reason.
6. **0DTE IV is degenerate after the close** and the 0DTE rows in the all-expiries table inherit it; the multi-day table is the interpretable one.
7. **Deep-ITM rows are often closing trades, not bets** — a large theoretical value with V/OI near 1 and a big open interest is the signature.
8. **The universe is the most-traded names, not the whole market** — a relative screen over a fixed universe can only find anomalies inside it.
9. **Three populations, three named books.** The relative screen covers the 59-name relative-screen universe, the series book the 8 Pass-B mega-caps, and Pass A the published watchlist. Their ratios describe different populations and are not interchangeable — which is why every ratio in this file names its universe.
