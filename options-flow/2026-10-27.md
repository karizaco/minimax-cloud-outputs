## 0. Read this first — data-quality caveat

The skill's headline metric is V/OI, which by construction ranks *newly-opened, small* positions at the very top. On this run only **0 of 55 kept strikes (0.0%) have OI < 50**, and **0** kept rows sit ≥ 20% away from spot.

**Four data-path caveats materially affect this file; two are bugs I found in my own pipeline and corrected, and two are defects in the upstream data or in the earlier files of this series:**

1. **Expiry dates were off by one day in my first pass, and I corrected them.** Yahoo's `expirationDate` epoch is 00:00 **UTC** of the true expiry day, so rendering it in US/Eastern yields the previous calendar day. The OCC contract symbol encodes the date unambiguously (`AAPL261028C*` = 2026-10-28) and is what this file now keys on. Every expiry and DTE below comes from the contract symbol, not from a timezone conversion.
2. **Yahoo drops the just-expired session-date 0DTE from `expirationDates` after the close.** The 2026-10-27 expiry is still absent from every Yahoo list hours after the 16:00 ET close even though those contracts traded heavily. The independent Cboe delayed chain still carries it, so the 0DTE slice below is sourced from Cboe and the rest from Yahoo. **Cboe is a different venue's tape, not Yahoo's** — it is used here to fill a gap Yahoo creates, and the two sources are cross-validated against each other in section 4.
3. **The ITM/OTM column was inverted on every call row in the earlier files of this series, and is fixed here.** The prior artifacts derived the label from the sign of (strike − spot) alone, which is wrong for calls: a call whose strike sits *above* spot is out-of-the-money, not in-the-money. The 2026-10-23 file, for instance, labelled a GOOGL 400 call (spot 320.21) as "ITM +24.9%" when it is 24.9% **out** of the money. Labels in this file are derived side-aware and are validated against Yahoo's own `inTheMoney` field on all 1,037 Yahoo-sourced rows (1,037/1,037 agree), and independently against Cboe's delta field. **The prior files' volume, V/OI and bias figures are unaffected — only the ITM/OTM text labels and the DTE dates were wrong.**
4. **The skill's `expiries=4` window is asymmetric across underlyings.** Yahoo's listed expiries differ per ticker (IWM lists daily weeklies; MSFT/GOOGL start later), so the four-expiry window is not the same set of calendar dates for every underlying. This is upstream behaviour, not a filter applied here.

The **0 of 55** figure above is a selection artifact, not evidence of a pristine book. The per-underlying top-10 cut is made on **volume**, which by construction favours large positions carrying large open interest, and it is the cut that removes the fragile rows: the parsed pool holds 1100 rows (volume ≥ 100) of which 389 clear the V/OI, volume and DTE filters, and 42 of those have OI < 50. The top-10 cut keeps 55 of them and the lowest open interest it admits is 102. **The OI floor in section 2b therefore removes nothing today and section 2b is identical to section 1 by construction** — that is a property of this run's volume cut, not a statement that the underlying book has no small positions in it.

### Selection sensitivity — the tag depends on how the per-underlying top-10 is chosen

The skill calls each underlying with `top_n=10` but does not state what the ranking is. That choice materially changes the answer, because the call/put volume sums behind the bias tag are computed over whatever survives the cut:

| Per-underlying top-10 selected by | n kept | Call vol | Put vol | P/C | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Volume** (used here) | 55 | 1,249,570 | 762,286 | 0.61 | **CALL** |
| V/OI | 64 | 409,177 | 345,927 | 0.85 | MIXED |

The V/OI cut keeps only 755,104 contracts versus 2,011,856 for the volume cut — it drops **62.5% of kept volume**. V/OI-based pre-selection is self-referential (it preferentially keeps rows with small open interest, which is what inflates V/OI) and it discards the largest lines. **The tag reported above follows the volume cut** because it retains the flow that actually happened; the V/OI cut is shown here so the difference is visible rather than buried.
