**Yahoo's options chain was unavailable this run (HTTP 429 on the crumb handshake), so Cboe is the sole chain source.** Every prior file in this series was built on the Yahoo v7 chain with Cboe used for validation or gap-fill; today the order is inverted. A full cross-check against an independent source was still possible and passed: **75 of 75 contracts match Nasdaq's option chain on open interest exactly**, and all 51 of those contracts that carry a Nasdaq volume print match Cboe exactly.

**Three data-path facts materially affect this file; all were measured, not assumed.**

1. **Cboe's `iv` field is a decimal fraction, not a percentage.** `iv: 0.3` means 30%, not 0.3%. Read literally, the IV column on this file would have been off by 100×. The unit was confirmed by inverting Black-Scholes against each contract's mid-quote: across **22,356 contracts with an identifiable mid-quote vol**, the median absolute gap between Cboe's IV and the inverted vol is **1.0 vol point** (within 5 points on 75.4% of contracts). On the kept set the two agree to within ~1–3 points wherever both exist. IV in this file is Cboe's, displayed as a percentage.
2. **Today's top V/OI row is a 1-contract open interest, and that is a structural property of the metric, not a data error.** The #1 row (GOOGL 287.5 call, 2026-10-30) traded **24,376 contracts against an open interest of 1**. Cboe's snapshot carries *yesterday's* settled open interest against *today's* session volume, so on a day when a name gaps −12.6% and fresh positions open all session, V/OI is inflated by construction at exactly the rows where fresh money shows up. The headline ranking therefore measures **newly-opened, small positions**, not institutional size. An explicit OI floor is reported in section 2b: only **1 of 68** kept rows (1.5%) sits below OI 50, so the rest of the book is not fragile — but the first five rows of the ranking all sit under OI 320.
3. **This book's shape inverted versus the last two runs: 0DTE is no longer the centre.** The prior two files were dominated by the session-date expiry (82.3% of kept volume on 2026-10-28). Today only **IWM and GLD carry a 2026-10-29 expiry** — the other six underlyings' Friday expiry (2026-10-30) is their nearest, so **68.9% of kept volume sits at DTE 1** and just 30.9% at DTE 0. The book is now *next-day* positioning, which on this calendar is the day after a Fed decision and the day of Alphabet's −12.6% collapse, and the day before Amazon's print.

### Selection sensitivity — the tag depends on how the per-underlying top-10 is chosen

The skill calls each underlying with `top_n=10` but never states the ranking. That choice changes the answer, because the call/put volume sums behind the bias tag are computed over whatever survives the cut — and today the choice **flips the tag outright**.

| Per-underlying top-10 selected by | n kept | Call vol | Put vol | P/C | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Volume** (used here) | 68 | 1,594,336 | 1,194,396 | 0.75 | **CALL** |
| V/OI | 69 | 240,042 | 652,949 | 2.72 | PUT |

The V/OI cut keeps 892,991 contracts versus 2,788,732 for the volume cut — it drops **68.0% of kept volume**. That is the self-referential trap: V/OI pre-selection preferentially keeps rows with small open interest, which is exactly what inflates V/OI, and the small-OI rows are also the small-volume rows. **On 2026-10-28 the two cuts agreed on the tag (CALL / MIXED-leaning); today they disagree.** The tag reported above follows the volume cut because it retains the flow that actually happened; the V/OI cut is shown so the disagreement is visible rather than buried. Anyone reading this file as "call-leaned" should know that a defensible alternative selection of the same raw data says the opposite.
