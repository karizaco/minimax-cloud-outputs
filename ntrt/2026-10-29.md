## Catalysts — the deep dives

Per the skill's cap, deep dives are limited to **five** names; every other row above carries a
one-line attribution from the same morning's coverage.

1. **GOOGL (-11.7%) — the day the market asked whether AI eats search.** Q3 revenue of $127.58B
   (+25% y/y) came in below the ~$128B consensus and **adjusted EPS of $2.12 missed the $2.94
   expected**. The detail that mattered was **Search and other revenue: +12% to $63.35B, roughly
   $1.5B short of estimates, decelerating from +17% in Q2 and +19% in Q1** — the first hard
   evidence that AI answers cannibalize the ad business. Google said query growth was "the highest
   it's been in the last five years," and CFO-level commentary pointed at **lower monetization in
   AI Mode**, where each AI-answer search currently earns less than a classic one, plus FX as the
   biggest reported factor. Google Cloud grew 103% to $30.7B with a 36% operating margin and $561B
   in signed-but-unbilled contracts, so the AI payoff was working — it just wasn't paying for
   itself. **Free cash flow was -$9.1B** against an expected +$2.7B, with capex at $51.4B and the
   full-year guide kept at $195–205B (implying $63–73B in Q4), funded by $12.4B of stock and
   $28.7B of notes. The stock closed **-12.6%, its worst one-day decline since the 2004 IPO**,
   erasing roughly $500B of market value. UBS wrote that the result brings "Search cannibalization
   fears right back to the forefront."
2. **FTNT (-26.8%) — a raised revenue forecast and a cut services forecast.** Q3 revenue grew 20%
   and adjusted EPS of $0.86 beat, and the 2026 *product* revenue forecast was raised — but the
   **services-revenue forecast was cut**, and the market focused on a product line that analysts
   increasingly believe is approaching a turning point. The stock fell 26.4% pre-market and closed
   **-20.1%** at a five-month low; Barrons headlined it as a "record two-day rout" with "a key
   revenue driver" to blame. The cyber complex went with it: PANW, CRWD, OKTA, NET, ZS and SBRK
   all flagged lower on the day.
3. **NOW (-20.0%) — the forward-book metric, again.** Q3 revenue of $4.106B (+20.5%) came in
   marginally above the $4.09B consensus and the company **raised** full-year subscription revenue
   guidance to $15.78–15.79B and lifted its free-cash-flow-margin target to 36%. But adjusted EPS
   of $1.00 missed $1.02, and the **Q4 current-remaining-performance-obligation growth guide of
   18.5% fell short of the 19.8% expected** — from 20.5% in Q3 — with roughly 100bp of the
   shortfall attributed to a revenue mix shift into services and more termination-for-convenience
   clauses. It closed **-26.95%**, its worst session on record and a 14-year worst, erasing the
   year's gains; Cantor Fitzgerald called it an AI-driven buying opportunity. MarketWatch framed
   the whole episode as "the specter of AI disruption" returning to enterprise software.
4. **ALNY (-20.4%) — a beat with a demand warning and a reimbursement overhang.** Q3 revenue of
   $1.46B beat the $1.45B consensus and adjusted EPS of $16.26 beat, though that figure was
   inflated by a **~$1.9B non-cash tax benefit** from releasing valuation allowances; product
   revenue grew 52% to ~$1.29B on **AMVUTTRA at $1.13B (+65%)**, which itself missed the $1.15B

   to Hold, $318→$225, saying it is "hard to see a path for the stock with it not being a
   beat-and-raise story." It closed at $174.00, the lowest close of the year.
5. **GKOS (+16.3%) and CROX (+16.8%) — the two clean beats on the tape.** Glaukos reported revenue

## Themes

**The market's question changed overnight, and it was about monetization, not capability.**
Alphabet's Cloud grew 103% and it had 1 billion Gemini users — and the stock still lost 12.6%,
because the business that funds the build-out, Search advertising, decelerated to 12% and the
free cash flow went to -$9.1B. Friday's rout (see `ntrt/2026-10-30.md`) was the next chapter of the
same question, triggered by Intel. **The two sessions should be read as one event:** the market
began asking what AI returns on hundreds of billions of capex, and the answer came back "not yet."

**Software was repriced on forward-book metrics while its quarter was fine.** NOW, FTNT, GOOGL —
each raised or held a headline guide and each was sold. The metric that mattered was the one that
looks forward: cRPO growth, services revenue, search growth, free cash flow. Enterprise software
had spent a year being bought for AI optionality; on Thursday the market started charging for it.

**Health care split violently into "own problem" and "not own problem."** ALNY (-25.1%) and IOVA
(-35.5%) fell on company-specific demand and trial news while the **health care sector rose 2.4%**
and Gilead gained 4.0%. ALNY's decline is the third leg of a pattern: a beat on revenue, a guide
to the low end, and a policy overhang (Medicare Advantage step-therapy, then CMS pricing).

**The bid was genuinely idiosyncratic on Thursday.** GKOS, CROX, PRCH, ADT and a small group of
earnings names carried it, with ADT's take-private at $10.00/share cash (a 45.6% premium to the
Oct 27 close, $7.6B equity value) the day's cleanest event. That is a different tape from
Friday's, where the whole complex moved together.

## Needs human eyes

- **The services-revenue cut at Fortinet is the most underappreciated line on the tape.** A raised
  product guide and a cut services guide is a *mix* deterioration, not a demand problem — and it is
  the first crack in the firewall story that carried the cyber complex.
- **ALNY's three problems are not independent and not all temporary.** Demand guidance to the low
  end, a Medicare Advantage step-therapy policy from Oct 1, and a pending CMS rule are three
  separate pressures on the same franchise, and the 2027 guidance was pushed to February precisely
  when the company would normally be giving it. H.C. Wainwright called the selloff an
  overreaction; Stifel did not. The gap between those two calls is the trade.
- **IOVA's read-through risk is trial design, not the response rate.** A 24.6% response rate was
  called "consistent" with the prior 25.6% — but the market wanted a new study in biomarker-selected
  patients, which is a different and harder population.
- **A pre-market gap can fade before the open, and on this tape it often did.** PGY gapped -16.7%
  pre-market and closed -1.7%. The pre-market bars count is in the table for this reason: RES (3
  bars), PWP (4 bars), GBFH (9 bars), ALKT (9 bars), OSK (13 bars) and ALNY (14 bars) are gaps wide
  enough to matter and thin enough to be a quote rather than a trade.
- **Reconstruction cannot see a name that gapped and reversed before the open.** See the header
  caveat: the universe was seeded from Oct-29 opening and session moves. A pre-market-only mover

## Method & caveats

1. **This is a reconstruction, not a live capture.** The original 09:00 ET run died on the Token
   Plan quota wall (error 2067) — the delivery auditor's `audit/status` branch records the
   2026-10-29 run of this task (session 457629827952876) with status 2 and the 2067 message, and no
   `ntrt/2026-10-29.md` existed on any ref. This resumed instance rebuilt the brief from **Yahoo's
   archived 5-minute pre/post bars** (`v7/finance/spark`, `includePrePost=true`, `range=5d`, 20
   symbols per request), taking the last pre-market bar before 09:30 ET as the pre-market print,
   the Oct-28 15:55 bar as the reference close, and then re-anchoring the gap to the **official**
   Oct-28 close from shibui.
2. **The reconstruction was independently spot-checked.** A direct re-read of GOOGL's Oct-29
   pre-market series returned 66 bars ending at 09:25 ET with a last price of $286.94 — matching
   the sweep output exactly — and the reconstructed prices for names carried by published premarket
   tables agreed to the cent (CVEO $36.00, CDNA $82.00, AGIO $32.00 on the following session).
   The reconstruction is sound; it is a replay of a real tape.
3. **The universe is seeded, not scanned live.** US common stocks with ≥$200M market cap and
   Oct-29 volume ≥10,000 were ranked by Oct-29 opening gap and session move; 539 candidates (open
   gap ≥2% or session move ≥6%) were measured for their actual pre-market gap, yielding **193 names
   at ≥3%** (54 up / 139 down). **Names that gapped pre-market and faded before the open can be
   missing.**
4. **120 of the 539 candidates returned no pre-market bars** (illiquid names that did not print
   pre-open) and are excluded as `no_data`, not as zero gaps.
5. **Pre-market volume is not available.** Yahoo's spark endpoint returns `close` only for these
   bars and the chart endpoint reports volume 0 on pre-market bars, so this artifact carries **no
   "PM volume" or "% of ADV" column** — the pre-market bars count is the honest thinness proxy.
6. **Relative volume is a daily proxy.** No intraday RVOL is computed; closing session volume and
   the 10-day average are in the raw row set.
7. **The `ntrt_mtrt` backend and the MCP finance tools are both absent.** `GET
   http://127.0.0.1:8000/health` refused connection and no `NTRT_BASE_URL` is set, so skill Mode A
   was unavailable; `mcp__tradingview_advanced__*` and `mcp__yahoo_finance__*` are not registered in
   this runtime (verified 2026-10-28), so the skill's Mode B screener was replaced by the public
   equivalents above. **This is one fallback, not two** — no MCP path was invoked, so it produced
   zero runtime failures of its own.
8. **Catalyst coverage is deliberately uneven and marked as such.** Five names carry researched
   catalysts; the rest carry a one-line attribution or an explicit "no confirmed catalyst" mark.
   Where no cause was identified, no cause is inferred.
