## Themes

**The market repriced the return on AI capex, not the demand for it.** Alphabet's search-advertising
miss on Oct 29 (stock -12.6%, its worst day on record, roughly $500B of market value) and Amazon's
earnings the same night with trailing-12-month free cash flow at **-$42.4B** formed one argument:
the industry is spending faster than it can show returns. The Nasdaq Composite fell **2.86%**
(worst day since June 5), the SOX **7.2%**, and the Nasdaq-100 erased over $1T. Intel's 17
price-target cuts turned a single-name disappointment into a sector event.

**The transmission ran through the physical supply chain, not the software layer.** The names that
gapped hardest were not AI applications — they were the companies that physically build the
datacenter: server makers (DELL -12.4%, HPQ -15.1%), semi-equipment (UCTT -10.4%, AEHR -10.5%, ONTO
-9.3%), connectivity (MXL -9.3%, AAOI -9.3%) and power (BE -8.9%). Software that *sells* into the
AI build-out (GDYN +10.3%, RMNI +20.3%, ASUR +21.3%) gapped **up**.

**Two credit stories ran underneath the AI story.** ABR's dividend suspension and BBUC's collapse
were mortgage-REIT events with their own causes, and both sat inside a market-wide risk-off tape
where they were easy to mis-file as AI read-through. **A screen that excludes REITs cannot see
them at all** — which is exactly how the first edition of this brief lost ARI, the day's largest
mover, and ABR.

**Guidance, once again, was the dividing line — and it cut in both directions.** INTC, TWLO, ILMN
and RDDT all beat the quarter and were sold. OSPN, CDNA, GDYN, RMNI and ASUR beat and were bought.
TWLO is the cleanest example: it beat Q3 *and* raised full-year guidance, and still fell 23.5%
pre-market because Q4 organic growth was guided to 11–12% from 16%.

## Method & caveats

1. **This is a reconstruction, not a live capture.** The original 09:00 ET run died on the Token Plan
   quota wall (error 2067) — the delivery auditor's `audit/status` branch records this session
   (457983151079669) with status 2 and the 2067 message, and no `ntrt/2026-10-30.md` existed on any
   ref. This resumed instance rebuilt the brief from the **TradingView public screener**, whose
   `premarket_*` columns still hold the 2026-10-30 session because the market has not reopened
   since.
2. **This edition corrects two defects in the first edition, and the corrections are material.**
   The first edition seeded its universe from shibui filtered on `type = 'Common Stock'`, which
   **excluded REITs** and dropped 178 names including **ARI (-63.9%)**, the day's largest mover. It
   also read prices from Yahoo's archived bars, which **carry no pre-market volume** and go
   **stale** for thinly-traded names — 13 of the first edition's top 40 had a stale Yahoo tape, and
   ADT's stale print ($7.46) understated a stock that opened +33%. TradingView screens
   `type = stock`, which includes REITs, and supplies volume for all 523 names.
3. **Independent validation.** The published Oct-30 pre-market table (StockAnalysis, retrieved
   2026-11-01) agrees to the cent on every overlapping name: ARI -63.88% at $2.33, ABOS -85.15% at
   $0.34 (below the $200M floor and therefore excluded here), ATEC -39.8%, INTC -25.7%,
   RDDT -22.8%, BBUC -23.26% at $22.01, TWLO -20.9%, ABR -18.5%, OABI -17.2%. `market-recap.get_quotes`
   confirms the settled Oct-30 closes with an `as_of` stamp of 2026-10-30T20:00:01Z, and shibui's
   official Oct-29 closes are the gap denominator.
4. **TradingView's pre-market snapshot is one moment, not the last trade.** On fast names it
   differs from the published table by 1–4pp (NPKI: TV -27.7% vs published -23.7%); the published
   table confirms TV on the overlapping names. The Yahoo pre-market range is published **only where
   it agrees with TV within 0.6%**, so a stale range can never appear next to a correct price.
5. **Relative volume is a 30-day daily proxy.** `average_volume_30d_calc` is a trailing 30-session
   average, not an intraday measure; the "% of ADV" column is PM volume against that average, which
   is the honest importance measure and the same construction the 2026-10-28 brief used.
6. **The `ntrt_mtrt` backend and the MCP finance tools are both absent.** `GET
   http://127.0.0.1:8000/health` refuses connection and no `NTRT_BASE_URL` is set, so skill Mode A
   was unavailable; `mcp__tradingview_advanced__*` and `mcp__yahoo_finance__*` are not registered in
   this runtime (confirmed again 2026-11-01: catalog 133 tools, 0 finance), so the skill's Mode B
   screener was reached through its **public HTTPS endpoint** rather than the MCP tool. This repo's
   own `ntrt/_pipeline/fetch_screen.py` already does exactly that, and it is the source this
   edition uses.
7. **Catalyst coverage is deliberately uneven and marked as such.** Five names carry researched
   catalysts; the rest carry a one-line attribution or an explicit "no confirmed catalyst" mark.
   Where no cause was identified, no cause is inferred. Deep dives are capped at five per the skill
   spec.
8. **Index levels for the session are from the reconstructed tape, not this brief.** SPY -1.77%
   ($755.72), QQQ -3.47% ($690.49), Nasdaq Composite -2.86% (25,437.00), from `market-recap` and
   press.
