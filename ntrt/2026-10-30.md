## Themes

**The market repriced the return on AI capex, not the demand for it.** Alphabet's search-advertising
miss on Oct 29 (stock -12%, its worst day on record, roughly $500B of market value), Amazon's
earnings the same night with trailing-12-month free cash flow at **-$42.4B** and capex up $94.8B, and
Intel's guide together formed one argument: the industry is spending faster than it can show
returns. The Nasdaq Composite fell **2.86%** (worst day since June 5), the SOX **7.2%**, and the
Nasdaq-100 erased over $1T. Intel's 17 price-target cuts turned a single-name disappointment into a
sector event.

**The transmission ran through the physical supply chain, not the software layer.** The names that
gapped hardest were not AI applications — they were the companies that physically build the
datacenter: server makers (DELL -11.8%, HPQ -13.6%, HPE -7%), semi-equipment (UCTT -10.4%, AEHR
-10.6%, ONTO -9.3%, AMAT -5%, LRCX -5.5%), connectivity (MXL -9.4%, SMTC -9.8%, AAOI -9.3%) and
power (BE -8.9%). Software that *sells* into the AI build-out (GDYN +10.3%, RMNI +20.3%, ASUR
+21.3%) gapped **up**.

**Two consumer-internet names broke on their own numbers, not the macro.** RDDT's DAU miss and
login-only pivot, and ATEC's guidance cut, had nothing to do with AI capex and would have been
material on any tape. RDDT fell 29% on a quarter that beat on both revenue and EPS.

**Guidance, once again, was the dividing line — and it cut in both directions.** INTC, TWLO, ILMN
and RDDT all beat the quarter and were sold. OSPN, CDNA, GDYN, RMNI and ASUR beat and were bought.
TWLO is the cleanest example of the pattern in the tape: it beat Q3 *and* raised full-year guidance,
and still fell 21.5% pre-market because Q4 organic growth was guided to 11–12% from 16%.

**The bid rotated to defensives.** Consumer staples +1.4%, energy +1.9%, health care +0.7%, with
Coca-Cola, Walmart, Eli Lilly and Berkshire each up more than 1% and Tesla's weakness pulling the
Nasdaq down further. Money moved from AI beta to cash-generative large caps — a rotation, not a
flight from equities.

## Method & caveats

1. **This is a reconstruction, not a live capture.** The original 09:00 ET run died on the Token
   Plan quota wall (error 2067) — the delivery auditor's `audit/status` branch records this session
   (`457983151079669`) with status 2 and the 2067 message, and no `ntrt/2026-10-30.md` existed on any
   ref. This resumed instance rebuilt the brief from **Yahoo's archived 5-minute pre/post bars**
   (`v7/finance/spark`, `includePrePost=true`, `range=5d`, 20 symbols per request), taking the last
   pre-market bar before 09:30 ET as the pre-market print and the Oct-29 15:55 bar as the reference
   close, then re-anchoring the gap to the **official** Oct-29 close from shibui.
2. **The reference close was cross-validated two ways.** shibui's official Oct-29 closes and
   `market-recap.get_quotes` (settled, `as_of` 2026-10-30T20:00:01Z) agree with the reconstructed
   tape, and the reconstructed **pre-market prices** agree with independent published premarket
   tables to the cent on the names those tables carry (CDNA $82.00, AGIO $32.00, ILMN $255.00,
   CVEO $36.00, OSPN +23%). The reconstruction is sound; it is a replay of a real tape.
3. **The universe is seeded, not scanned live.** 2,488 US common stocks with ≥$200M market cap and
   Oct-30 volume ≥10,000 were ranked by Oct-30 opening gap and session move; 759 candidates (open
   gap ≥2% or session move ≥6%) were then measured for their actual pre-market gap, yielding **366
   names at ≥3%** (28 up / 338 down). A live 09:00 ET screen would rank all 2,488 on the pre-market
   print; this ranks a 759-name subset. **Names that gapped pre-market and faded before the open
   can be missing.**
4. **Zero real movers were lost to the sweep.** 160 of the 759 candidates returned no pre-market
   bars (illiquid names that did not print pre-open). **None of the 160 had an opening gap ≥5%**, so
   no large mover was dropped for want of a pre-market print.
5. **Pre-market volume is not available.** Yahoo's spark endpoint returns `close` only for these
   bars and the chart endpoint reports volume 0 on pre-market bars, so the artifact carries **no
   "PM volume" or "% of ADV" column** — the pre-market bars count is the honest thinness proxy.
   Unlike the live briefs, this artifact cannot distinguish a 20-share quote from a 200,000-share
   trade, only from a one-bar print.
