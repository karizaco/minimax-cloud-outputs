## Method, QC and caveats

**Screen definition.** TradingView public screener, `markets: ["america"]`, columns `premarket_change` and `market_cap_basic`, filter `premarket_change` in `[3, 1000]` **or** `[−1000, −3]` **and** `market_cap_basic > 200000000`, sorted by `premarket_change` and then re-sorted locally by absolute gap. Two separate screen calls (one ascending, one descending) because the scanner's numeric filter operator `greater_or_equal` is rejected with `Unsupported num operation` — `in_range` is the working operator. Universe census 20,088; 7,547 above the cap; 136 gainers and 46 decliners at the ±3% threshold; 106 rows carried (top 60 gainers, all 46 decliners).

**Reference-close test (drift-proof).** The validity test is whether both feeds agree on the *reference close* — Monday's regular-session close — not on the percentage. TradingView's `close` was compared with Yahoo's last completed-session close. **27 of 27 comparable rows agree exactly (0.000%).** A live market moving between two snapshots cannot produce a false alarm on this test, which a direct percentage comparison could.

**Second test — open/mark magnitude.** The pre-open mark is compared against the second feed's own last pre-open print, both expressed as a percentage of the same reference close. The spread column is that disagreement. It is banded, not pass/fail, because the two feeds sample the pre-open book at different seconds.

**Third test — internal consistency.** For every carried row, `premarket_close / (1 + premarket_change/100)` must equal `close` within 0.5%. **0 failures across all 106 carried rows.** This is what kills 10x artefacts and stale-reference rows: on the previous run of this cron it rejected a row printing −91.24% against an implied $53.90 reference while its own close was $5.39.

**Two-read discipline.** The screen was read four times (09:04:47, 09:05:13, 09:07:14, 09:08:27 ET) and Yahoo three times (09:06:55, 09:07:27, 09:08:40 ET). Read-to-read spread on the screener: median 0.00pp; maxima 1.52pp (reads 1→2, INIO), 2.76pp (reads 2→3, ARTV), 1.05pp (reads 3→4, ITW). Yahoo: median 0.00–0.03pp; maxima 0.65pp (ARTV) and 1.30pp (KNSA). Roster churn between adjacent reads was one ticker in and one out (`ARLO` out, `WULF` in). Single pre-open prints are not published as if stable.

**QC labels used in the tables.**
- `cross-checked` — both feeds agree on the reference close, the second feed's pre-open print is < 45 min old, and pre-market volume ≥ 20,000 shares.
- `stale print` — the second feed's last pre-open print is more than 45 minutes old (`HWKN` 90 min, `SXC` 123 min, `PII` 95 min, `ISD` 81 min). The mark may be correct but it is not current.
- `thin book` — fewer than 20,000 shares traded pre-open, so the mark is a handful of prints. 16 of the 40 published rows fall here.
- `home-listing only` — the US line has no Yahoo pre-open bar, so Yahoo resolves to the home exchange (a different session) and the US mark is single-source. 8 rows.
- `no second-feed mark` — the row is inside the cross-check batch and its reference close was confirmed, but the second feed has no pre-open print for it at all (`YDES`). 1 row.
- `not cross-checked` — outside the 30-row cross-check batch. 10 rows.
- `REF MISMATCH` — a reference-close disagreement above 0.5%. **Zero rows triggered this.**

**Known limitations of this run.**
