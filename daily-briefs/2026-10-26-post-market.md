## TL;DR

Stocks closed higher with a clear AI-led rotation: the S&P 500 gained 0.70% to 7,676.77 and the Nasdaq Composite added 0.82% to 26,169.38, both snapping a two-session slide. The catalyst was Anthropic's public S-1 filing (Nasdaq ticker "ANT"), which revived enthusiasm for the artificial-intelligence trade and pulled semiconductor and optical-networking names sharply higher — the SOXX ETF rose 2.85% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index gained more than 3%. Volatility compressed hard, with the VIX down 5.45% to 18.39 and the VXN down 6.71% to 26.40, while the Cboe equity put/call ratio printed 0.60.

The day's dominant negative was geographic rather than sectoral: Brazil's president Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth term in Sunday's runoff over Flávio Bolsonaro, unwinding the "Bolsonaro trade" that had rallied Brazilian assets. The iShares MSCI Brazil ETF (EWZ) fell 19.82%, and US-listed Brazilian financials suffered far worse — XP −48.36%, BBD −28.01%, ITUB −20.37%, NU −16.95%. São Paulo trading was briefly halted by a circuit breaker, the first such halt since 2020.

Two additional setups sit under tomorrow's tape: the FOMC decision lands Wednesday at 2:00 PM ET with Microsoft and Alphabet reporting after that close, and Visa, Seagate, Coca-Cola, Boeing and Robinhood report Tuesday.

## Open research queue

1. **Brazil's fiscal reaction function** — the selloff is priced on fiscal discipline doubts, not on realised earnings. The next datapoints are Lula's finance-minister nomination, the 2027 budget, and the real's path. The "Bolsonaro has not conceded" overhang adds a legal-and-political tail that is not in any model. Watch the ADR-to-local-basis relationship for signs the Bovespa circuit breaker has truncated the discovery process.
2. **Anthropic's economics** — the S-1 disclosed $16.29 billion of first-half 2026 revenue against an $80.7 billion net loss. If the IPO prices in the mid-November window, the terms will tell the market what revenue-per-dollar-of-AI-capex actually looks like, which is the precise question behind last week's AI selloff.
3. **Optical capacity as the AI bottleneck** — Lumentum management says optoelectronics are sold out through 2029 and that roughly 70% of demand cannot be met next year. New capacity from the Greensboro fab and the expanded Japan fab does not arrive until 2029-2030. Worth stress-testing whether the market is extrapolating scarcity rents rather than a normalisation path.
4. **Neocloud financing** — Elon Musk's claim on X that debt markets have shut off funding for data-centre operators without independent revenue has been weighing on CRWV, NBIS, HUT, IREN, CIFR and WULF. This is an unverified social-media claim that nevertheless moved real assets; the open question is whether any of these names actually faces a refinancing wall in the next two quarters.
5. **VIX at 18.39 into a Fed decision** — vol has been compressed while a policy decision and two megacap earnings land inside 48 hours. The 0.60 equity put/call ratio says the market is not paying for that convexity. Whether that is complacency or correct is a one-way bet that resolves Wednesday afternoon.
6. **The communication-services split** — XLC fell 1.57% while XLK rose 1.69% on the same day, with the same AI capital flowing in one direction and out of the other. Meta's $95 billion raise is the load-bearing fact: it suggests the hyperscalers are funding the buildout with equity rather than cash flow, which changes the risk profile of the trade regardless of Anthropic's listing.
