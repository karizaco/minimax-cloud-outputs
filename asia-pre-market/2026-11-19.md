# Asia pre-market reactions — Thursday, 19 November 2026

## TL;DR

- **Asia split hard, and the split is the story.** Taiwan had the session's only crash — the TAIEX fell **1,768.02 points to 38,426.89 (-4.40%)**, its seventh-largest one-day closing decline on record, with 128 TWSE limit-downs and three sessions down 5,248 points. Korea went the other way (**KOSPI +1.39%** to 5,137.89) on the Korea-China trade deal, Japan was flat on the surface and positive underneath (**Nikkei +0.71%** to 59,444.21 while the TOPIX slipped -0.04%), and Hong Kong fell for a third straight day (**Hang Seng -1.95%** to 22,841.62).
- **The most consequential market is Taiwan, and it is a de-rating, not a catalyst.** Nvidia's beat did not lift the AI supply chain — it broke it: US export controls dated 15 Nov, doubts about AI capex returns, a hawkish Fed and three straight days of foreign selling took the TAIEX below its 40,000 handle and its annual moving average. Every one of Taiwan's 15 published rows is a limit-down price. Taiwan's FSC answered after the close with "short-selling restrictions 2.0" from 20 Nov.
- **The caveat that changes how to read the boards: the two biggest numbers in this brief are artefacts.** Hong Kong's `HKEX:6656` Sigenergy printed **-65.80%** and `HKEX:659` CTF Services **-18.58%** — both are corporate-action days, and their real moves are **+2.60%** and **-6.93%**. Both were corrected before the 15-per-direction cap was applied: Sigenergy drops out of the board entirely on the corrected basis, and CTF Services is published at -6.93%. Separately, Investing.com's TAIEX of 38,347.67 (-4.6%) is an intraday print: the settled close is 38,426.89 (-4.40%).

**US read-across (what this means at 09:30 ET).** The overnight message is not "Asia sold off" — it is "the AI trade split by geography". Korea's substrate and MLCC complex bid on Nvidia's own expansion announcement while Taiwan's AI hardware complex hit the limit, and the US-listed proxies are already pricing that divergence rather than Taiwan's crash. The US session therefore opens with the Taiwan de-rating only partly discounted, and with Hong Kong's internet earnings landing into it.

- **Exposed US names / ADRs:** TSM **+1.15% on 205,412 shares** (material) — the ADR is *not* following Taipei's -3.45%; SKHY (SK hynix ADR) **+5.54% on 267,348** (material) — the Korean hardware bid is what the US is pricing; BABA **-1.56% on 1,246,016** (material) against Alibaba's -5.51% in Hong Kong ahead of its results after the HK close. Below the 50,000-share floor: JD +1.40% (29,908), HSAI +1.85% (20,523), MNSO -3.93% (8,123) — no material US pre-market print yet on any of them. Taiwan's AI names (ASPEED, Unimicron, Win Semiconductors, Global Unichip) have **no direct US-listed proxy** at all.
- **Exposed US sectors:** Electronic Technology / Semiconductor is the transmission channel and it is split, not directional — SK hynix's ADR is up 5.54% pre-market on Korea's substrate/MLCC expansion while the Taiwan AI chain has no US line to express its -10% limit-downs. Retail Trade / China internet is the second channel: BABA is the only materially printed ADR for a Hang Seng that fell for a third session on Tencent's post-results -6.55% and Alibaba's pre-results -5.51%. The two US-side drivers behind the Asian de-rating are the Fed's hawkish October minutes (some participants saw a 50bp December hike) and the 15 Nov export-control package.
- **The overnight implication:** the US has priced Korea's hardware bid and has *not* priced Taiwan's supply-chain capitulation — TSM is +1.15% pre-market on the day TSMC fell 3.45% in Taipei. That gap is the risk the 09:30 ET session carries on the Taiwan AI complex, and the Hong Kong internet results (Alibaba after the HK close, Tencent already out) land into it with only one ADR materially marked.
