# Asia pre-market reactions — Tuesday, 24 November 2026

## TL;DR

- **Asia ripped higher on one headline: Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, and the chip complex re-rated across the region.** Taiwan's TAIEX closed **+8.99%** at **40,122.35** (+3,310.46 points) — its **biggest single-day point gain on record** — Korea's KOSPI **+6.32%** to **5,006.03**, Tokyo's Nikkei **+3.91%** to **58,644.63** in its first session after the Labor Thanksgiving holiday, and the Hang Seng was the only market down, **-0.73%** at **22,219.65** (sixth straight decline).
- **Taiwan is the market that matters at the US open, and its rally is a price-locked, thin-tape bounce rather than a demand recovery.** **622 Taiwanese names closed at the +10% daily limit**, TSMC closed limit-up at NT$2,230 and added roughly 1,600 points, and the whole record gain came on turnover of just **NT$419.41bn — the lowest so far this year**. On a five-session basis the TAIEX is **+0.08%**: today's +8.99% only claws back last week's -17.5% (the worst week since 1991).
- **Caveat that changes how to read it: Asia chased a US move that the US pre-market is already giving back.** Nvidia closed Monday **+31.48%** at $165.05 on 776m shares and the Asian suppliers followed it the next morning; at 06:33 ET the US pre-market was **fading** the same complex — `TSM` **-1.85%** on 207,706 shares, `UMC` **-1.67%**, `ASX` **-3.39%** — with S&P 500 futures **-1.05%** at 7,178.25.

**US read-across.** Monday's US session set the catalyst: Nvidia closed +31.48% at $165.05 after 776.2m shares, and every Asian session on Tuesday traded that single headline. The US-listed ADRs of the Asian AI winners were already bid on Monday before any Asian cash market opened (`TSM` +14.08%, `UMC` +19.52%, `ASX` +21.36%, `MU` +19.04%, `IBIDY` +16.91%), so Tuesday's Asian gains largely echoed a move the US had already made — and this morning's US pre-market is giving part of it back.

- `TSM` $363.60 (Monday +14.08%), pre-market **-1.85%** on 207,706 shares; `UMC` $14.39 (Monday +19.52%) pre-market **-1.67%** on 190,642; `ASX` $31.88 (Monday +21.36%) pre-market **-3.39%** on 52,700 — the US complex is retracing, not extending.
- Korea is the un-priced leg: `KRX:307950` Hyundai AutoEver hit the +30% KOSDAQ limit, but the Korean names have no US ADR, and `SKM` (SK Telecom) is only +1.63% on 2,758 shares — too thin to quote.
- Hong Kong is the odd one out and it has an ADR: `BABA` pre-market **-3.29%** on 168,545 shares and `XPEV` **+1.84%** on 107,351 — Hong Kong's AI hardware fell while the mainland AI names bid, so the HK tape is not a China-tech read at all.

## Method, source health & caveats

**Method.** Eight TradingView per-country scanner pulls (4 markets × up/down), each with a `type=stock` filter, a ±2% change filter on the correct leg for each direction, a $2bn market-cap floor converted to local currency (JPY318bn at 159.07, TWD65.7bn at 32.84, HKD15.68bn at 7.8409, KRW2.698tn at 1,348.85), a local-close floor of 5, `range=[0,20000]`, and a per-direction truncation assert (rows returned must equal `totalCount` — 8/8 passed, so no pull was cut). Four Yahoo chart fetches supplied each index's session date, close and 5-session change; one `america/scan` pull (`type=dr`, 1,611 rows) supplied the US ADR read-across; the MCP-over-HTTP bridge (`market-recap`) supplied the US closes, futures, gold and yields; a 93-symbol Yahoo chart sweep verified every published close and percentage against an independent series. Market status used the date-provenance rule: index session date (Test A) is primary, the archive comparison against the 23 November artifact (Test B) is the second proof, row count (Test C) is secondary only.

**Caveats.** Catalyst coverage is press-based: there is no structured Asian earnings calendar behind this routine, so a published "no company-specific catalyst identified in the sources checked" means no catalyst was found in the sources checked, not that none exists. The whole session is one macro headline — Nvidia's board reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO — and a macro driver is not a company event: the Taiwan and Korea limit clusters and the robotics complex are theme moves, which is why the events list carries only three company-specific catalysts. Korea's +6.32% came with a wide intraday range (open +3.64%, high 5,065.74, close 5,006.03) and a late institutional bid, so it is a recovered close. Taiwan's record gain on the year's lowest turnover is a price-locked tape; 622 limit-up names cannot be read as demand. US pre-market percentages are a 06:33 ET snapshot on live volume and will move before the 09:30 ET open; a pre-market percentage is quoted only where volume exceeds 50,000 shares, and the Japanese OTC ADRs (IBIDY, ATEYY) have no pre-market print at all. Index percentages come from the Yahoo chart bars, not from the bridge's `previous_close`, which is one session stale for `^HSI` (it printed 22,410.80 against the chart's 22,383.74). The 5-session column compares the last bar with the bar six bars back in the same fetch, so a holiday inside the window shifts the base bar by design. The 4-call catalyst cap was exceeded (8 press lookups plus the HK corporate-action screen) to source company-specific catalysts for the What-matters block rather than pad that list with unsourced rows; the true count is in the footer, as the v1.5 exception requires.
