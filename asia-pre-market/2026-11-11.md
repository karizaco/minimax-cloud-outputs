# Asia pre-market reactions — Wednesday, 11 November 2026

## TL;DR

- Asia ripped higher in an AI-hardware-led rally that went almost entirely against the tape it was reacting to: **TAIEX +3.62% to 44,257.15** (a 1,545.23-point day - the sixth-largest single-day closing point gain in index history, and a third straight 1,000+ point session), **KOSPI +2.60% to 5,375.36**, **Nikkei 225 +1.31% to 61,248.59**. The **Hang Seng was the only decliner, -0.98% to 24,315.92**. **93 published names**, 73 of them in the REACTION tier, with the buying concentrated in substrates, MLCC/passives, PCB and semiconductor equipment.
- **Taiwan is the most consequential market**, and also the most mechanically flattering one: TSMC +4.81% to NT$2,395 led a third straight 1,000+ point day, MediaTek +4.18% to NT$3,990, and the National Stabilization Fund made its tenth market-support intervention — but **14 of the 15 published Taiwan gainers are pinned at the +10% TWSE daily limit**, so the breadth is as much a price-limit artefact as a signal. Korea is the second most consequential: the KOSPI's +2.60% came from the AI-substrate complex (ISU Petasys +19.36%, Daeduck Electronics +13.88%), while the region's single largest mover was the opposite of a fundamental call — **Korean Air preferred at -24.77%**, having traded +24.51% earlier the same session.
- **The caveat that changes how to read it:** very little of this is independent earnings news, and the most dramatic numbers are the most likely to be misquoted — Taiwan's gainers are all at the +10% cap, **Korean Air preferred closed -24.77% after printing +24.51% earlier the same day**, and **Hong Kong published only 13 qualifying names in the entire market** (Fameglow Holdings +10.12% on a halt-and-resume history, with no company catalyst found in any source checked). Only **three** published rows carry a sourced, company-specific catalyst today: Dai-Dan, Hamamatsu Photonics and ISU Petasys — and the first two are Japanese interim results.

**US read-across.** The US pre-market has already priced the AI-hardware leg of this session and almost nothing else: TSM is +5.54% pre-market on 186,118 shares and the SK hynix ADR +7.97% on 339,547 shares, while every direct ADR line of today's actual movers is an OTC quote with no pre-market data at all. The macro backdrop the Asian tape ignored — a hot US CPI print, a hawkish Fed and Brent back near US$92 — is the backdrop that dragged Hong Kong, and the Hang Seng is the one Asian index that paid for it.

- **Exposed US names:** TSM (NYSE, Electronic Technology) closed $396.48 (+2.02% on 10 Nov) and is **+5.54% pre-market on 186,118 shares**; the SK hynix ADR (NASDAQ: SKHY) closed $129.66 (-3.51%) and is **+7.97% pre-market on 339,547 shares**; UMC (NYSE) is +5.14% on 86,204 shares. Every direct ADR line of today's actual movers is OTC with **no pre-market data** (SUMCO SUOPY, Kingboard Laminates KGBLY, IBIDEN IBIDY, Taiyo Yuden TYOYY, ASMPT ASMVY, Tokyu TOKUY, Sharp SHCAY, OMRON OMRNY). The one NYSE-line mover print is Honda (HMC) at **-4.30% on 3,400 shares** — below the 50,000-share materiality floor, i.e. no material print yet.
- **Exposed US sectors:** Electronic Technology / Semiconductor is the dominant exposure — Taiwan's AI-substrate, PCB and passive-component limit-up wave, Korea's substrate complex (ISU Petasys, Daeduck, Simtek) and Japan's imaging/semi-equipment bid (Hamamatsu, Ferrotec, IBIDEN, Taiyo Yuden) map to TSM, SKHY, UMC and the OTC ADR lines. Transportation is the second exposure — Korean Air preferred -24.77% and Hanjin KAL -14.64% on a control fight plus jet fuel at Brent near US$92 — and carries no material US print at 06:30 ET.
- **Overnight implication:** the US pre-market has priced the AI-hardware leg (TSM +5.54%, SKHY +7.97%, UMC +5.14%, all on material volume) and has **not** priced the divergence — the Hang Seng's -0.98% on a hot US CPI print and the Korean preferred-share control fight have no US line at all. The 09:30 ET session is where the two meet: a higher-yield, hawkish-Fed tape is the same tape that drew down Hong Kong.

## Flags

- **The Japanese regional prints are gone, and they were the biggest misquote risk on the table.** All 13 regional-venue rows in today's scan (1 Sapporo, 12 Nagoya) diverged from their Tokyo line and were resolved against the primary listing before publication: Takeda printed +23.26% on Sapporo against +1.21% on Tokyo, Daiwa Securities +21.03% on Nagoya against +2.35% on Tokyo, and NGK -33.40% on Nagoya against +3.18% on Tokyo. None of those numbers is published; a reader pulling them from a regional screen would be quoting the wrong line.
- **Taiwan's +10% cluster is a price limit, not a market.** 14 of the 15 published gainers close at or within 0.24pp of the TWSE +10% cap (only Asia Vital Components, +8.96%, is not limit-locked), and 6 of the 15 are TPEX (Taipei Exchange) listings rather than TWSE primary names. Treating 14 limit-ups as 14 independent re-ratings is the single easiest mistake to make with this table.
- **Korea's preferred lines are separate securities from their common lines.** Korean Air preferred (KRX:003495, -24.77%) and Korean Air common (KRX:003490, -2.93%) are different share classes of one issuer, as are Hanjin KAL preferred (-2.67%) and Hanjin KAL common (-14.64%). The preferred lines are the ones carrying the stake-battle and control-contest premium; they were pinned at the daily limit for six straight sessions into 9 Nov, so a multi-day series on these lines is a limit artefact rather than a series of fundamental re-ratings.
- **Hong Kong's table is not a representative sample of the market.** Only 13 names in the whole market cleared the screen (a 10-name up pool and a 3-name down pool against a HK$15.7bn market-cap floor). Fameglow Holdings +10.12% is the top name, and it carries a 23 Oct trading halt, a 2 Nov resumption, a 52-week range of HK$2.70-47.12 and no company-specific catalyst in any source checked.
- **A source conflict worth knowing about:** Investing.com reports the TAIEX at +3.11% and the MCP bridge reports +6.23%; both are wrong. The bridge's `previous_close` for ^TWII (41,661.70) and ^HSI (24,448.90) is one session stale, and six Taiwanese sources (Focus Taiwan/CNA, Taiwan News, ETtoday, NOWnews, FTNN, Capital Futures) all print 44,257.15 and +3.62%. The Hang Seng's -0.98% is press-rounded to -1.0%.
- **Two policy/flow drivers sit behind the Taiwan move and are not company events:** TSMC's NT$1bn stake in OpenAI and Anthropic (announced 10 Nov) and the National Stabilization Fund's tenth intervention of the year. The AI-hardware bid and the state bid are different things and should not be quoted as one.
- **The macro tape the Asian session ignored:** US October CPI +0.3% m/m, above expectations, with Fed officials hawkish on a December hike; Brent back to about US$92 after a two-month low; Nvidia -5.47% on 10 Nov. Asia rallied into it anyway on AI-hardware demand — the Hang Seng is the exception.
- **Taiwan's national stabilization fund and the Hong Kong flow split are not the same signal.** Offshore money was the seller in Hong Kong while the mainland rallied, which is why the Hang Seng fell while small-cap Hong Kong names rose.

## Method, source health & caveats

**Method.** TradingView's per-country scanner, eight calls (4 markets x up/down legs), with the up leg filtered `change > +2` and the down leg `change < -2`, a US$2bn market-cap floor converted to local currency (JPY 158.18, HKD 7.8449, KRW 1,339.00 from Frankfurter on 2026-11-11; TWD proxied at ~32.3 because Frankfurter carries no TWD), and a local-price floor of 5. `change` is read in percent units. Each market keeps the top 15 up and 15 down. The primary-listing rules were applied before the cap: the Japanese regional-venue rule (every NAG/SAPSE/FSE/SSEO row resolved against its TSE line and dropped if no TSE line exists), Hong Kong rows with a non-HKD currency dropped (2 rows), TPEX kept only where no TWSE listing of the same company exists (0 dropped), and Hong Kong dual-ticker dedupe on company name plus market cap (0 found today). Market status came from the index session date (Test A) corroborated by an archive comparison against the 2026-11-10 artifact (Test B): all four markets returned a session date of 2026-11-11 and an archive verdict of fresh, so all four published.

**Caveats.**

- **Catalyst lookups: 5, one over the skill's cap of 4.** Four were the per-market top-REACTION lookup; the fifth was added because the v1.5 `## What matters today` block requires three *sourced* company events and the four-call cap predates that section. Disclosed rather than silently taken.
