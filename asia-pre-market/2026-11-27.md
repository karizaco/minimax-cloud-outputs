# Asia pre-market reactions — Friday, 27 November 2026

## TL;DR
- Asia closed the week on an AI-and-semiconductor bid, not on a broad-market move: the Nikkei added 2.11% to 58,711.11 and the KOSPI 4.34% to 4,915.68, with almost every published Japanese and Korean name on the board a chip, memory, robot or power-equipment name.
- Korea was the most consequential session — the KOSPI's +4.34% came with the KOSDAQ up 8.13% past 700, a KRX buy-sidecar triggered at 10:22, and the whole board's top half rotating into brokers and humanoids on expectations of a Samsung in-house robot at CES 2027.
- Read the day as a rebound inside a violent three-day AI whipsaw, not as a new trend: the 5-session columns still show the Nikkei -1.23% and the TAIEX +9.09%, and the US 10-year near 4.92% is the live risk to the same trade that drove it.

**US read-across (09:30 ET).** Asia's move is one trade — semiconductors, storage, optical and humanoids — and the US has already started pricing it: Nasdaq futures were up more than 2% and the S&P 500 and Dow futures more than 1% at 08:15 GMT, after Wednesday's Nasdaq recovery and Nvidia's +31.48% on 23 November.
- **Exposed US lines (pre-market, 06:30 ET):** TSM (NYSE) +3.26% on 154,370 shares — TSMC rose 1.63% in Taipei; SKHY (NASDAQ) +6.62% on 542,180 shares — SK hynix rose 5.41% in Seoul; UMC (NYSE) +2.88% on 217,305 shares — note UMC *fell* 1.46% in Taipei, so the ADR bid is a US-side call, not a Taipei echo.
- **No US proxy for most of the board:** the Tokyo names (Lasertec, IBIDEN, SCREEN, Kioxia) carry only OTC ADRs with no material US pre-market print, and Compeq, Circuit Fabology, MiniMax and Z.AI have no listed US line at all — write "no direct US-listed proxy", not a stretched link.
- **Overnight implication:** the US session carries the risk, not Asia. Already priced is the AI/semiconductor bid; not priced is the Hong Kong-only AI-model pair and the Korean robot cluster, which have no US proxy. The setup is a cash open straight after an abbreviated Thanksgiving week with the 10-year near 4.92%, which is exactly what has been compressing AI data-centre financing costs.
