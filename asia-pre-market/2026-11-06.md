# Asia pre-market reactions — Friday, 06 November 2026

## TL;DR

- **What Asia did:** day two of the AI-hardware rout, and it got worse, not better. The Nikkei fell **-5.96%** to 57,434.77 and slid into a bear market, the KOSPI fell **-6.02%** to 5,059.69, the TAIEX fell **-4.51%** to 39,701.64, and the Hang Seng gave up **-1.27%** to 24,481.86 after yesterday's +3.67% rebound. Among Tokyo names above the $2bn floor only **8** cleared +2% against **482** decliners; the selloff was the market, not a few stocks. The trigger was the overnight US session — the Nasdaq's worst day in more than a year (-5.52%) and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index **-12.2%** — on fears that the Democratic midterm sweep will slow US data-centre build-out, with a possible December Fed *rate hike* on top (CME FedWatch ~70%).
- **Most consequential market: Korea.** The KRX triggered a **20-minute circuit breaker** three minutes into the session (KOSPI 4,924.61, -8.52%), a sell-sidecar was triggered on KOSDAQ, and the index closed at 5,059.69 (-6.02%) after a partial rebound — the second day of losses, following -5.28% on 5 Nov. The Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-Il waived the **140% collateral ratio** for brokers for three months to slow forced selling; foreigners sold heavily into the chip complex, with SK hynix (-10.44%) and Samsung Electronics (-6.35%) accounting for most of it.
- **The caveat that changes how to read it:** the biggest single prints on the board are **mechanical, not sentiment** — Japan Tobacco's -19.31% is an *untraded* limit-down close on Putin's seizure of JTI's Russian assets, all 15 Taiwan decliners sit within 0.15pp of the TWSE -10% limit, and Korea Investment's -17.34% is outside the KOSPI ±15% band, so those percentages are clipped rather than closing sentiment. Second, **TOPIX could not be sourced** (no free feed), so we cannot tell you whether Japan's move was broad or concentrated in the AI complex — and third, the US pre-market was already **bouncing 4-9% in AI hardware**, which prices a relief move the Asian close has not.

**US read-across (what this means at 09:30 ET).** The US session on 5 Nov took the full de-rating (Nasdaq -5.52%, SOX -12.2%, MU -17.46%, NVDA -9.26%, LRCX -16.36%, VRT -14.02%, TSM -10.78%), and the Asian close on 6 Nov is the *second* leg of the same trade rather than new information. What the Asian session adds is that the capitulation is now global and partly mechanical: Korea had to halt trading, Taiwan opened TSMC at its limit-down price, and Japan's largest single move is a state seizure, not a cycle. The US pre-market is already fading the panic — and that fade is priced **before** the ADRs have repriced for the Asian close, which is the gap that carries into the open.

- **Exposed US names / ADRs:** `SKHY` (SK hynix ADR) **+4.83%** pre-market on **574,244** shares — a material print, but it is a bounce, and it is not priced for Seoul: the KRX closed at 06:30 ET, roughly 20 minutes before this run, with SK hynix down 10.44%. `TSM` **+6.39%** on **227,565** shares against TSMC's -4.69% Taipei close; `ASX` (ASE Holding ADR) **+9.15%** on **88,548** shares against -7.87% in Taipei. `KT` +5.01% but on only **1,514** shares — no material US pre-market print yet; the same for `PUK` (-1.55%, 1,050 shares) and for the OTC lines of Lenovo (`LNVGY`), Hon Hai (`HNHPF`), ASMPT (`ASMVY`) and Kingboard Laminates (`KGBLY`), which printed no pre-market volume at all.
- **Exposed US sectors:** Electronic Technology / Semiconductor is the expression — MU **+9.06%**, NVDA **+4.72%**, VRT **+8.06%** on the Yahoo pre-market tape, with LRCX +10.2%, AMAT +7.4% and TER +7.2% on thinner volume (below the 50,000-share materiality bar for AMAT/TER). Producer Manufacturing (semiconductor equipment, Kingboard's CCL line) and the Hong Kong PCB/assembly chain (ASMPT, Kingboard) are the second-order exposure. There is **no direct US-listed proxy** for most of the 80 rows published here — the OTC ADR lines carry no pre-market volume, which is the honest answer rather than a stretched link.
- **The overnight implication:** the priced part is the *US* capitulation and a mechanical bounce; the un-priced part is the Asian close that the ADRs have not yet repriced for (SKHY, TSM, ASX all sit *above* the level their home-market closes imply). A benign US policy headline leaves the AI hardware leg bouncing and the Korean/Taiwan chip complex still repricing down at 09:30 ET; a fresh policy headline re-opens it. Note the **non-linear** risk: the US pre-market bounce is thin and unpriced relative to the Asian lows, so the open carries the gap, not the pre-market.

## Method, source health & caveats

**Method.** TradingView per-country scanner for the movers, closes and local-currency caps (8 calls: up/down for each of `japan`, `taiwan`, `hongkong`, `korea`), with the $2bn market-cap floor converted at the live FX rate (JPY 158.46, TWD 32.712, HKD 7.8452, KRW 1,344.69). Market status comes from **date provenance**: the last daily bar of each index (^N225, ^TWII, ^HSI, ^KS11) read in the exchange's own timezone — all four carry a **2026-11-06** bar, so all four markets traded on the fire date. The previous brief (2026-11-05) was fetched and compared row-by-row as the second, independent proof: `archive_compare_jp/tw/hk/kr=fresh` for all four. Every published close and percentage was then re-derived from Yahoo's own two-bar series — **76 of the 80 published rows matched exactly, zero mismatches**; the four exceptions are the TPEX rows above. US exposure was mapped by joining the day's movers against the TradingView US ADR universe (1,632 ADRs, `type=dr`) and the US large-cap universe (11,751 names, `type=stock`), with pre-market prints taken from the TradingView scanner and corroborated against Yahoo's pre/post-market minute tape.
