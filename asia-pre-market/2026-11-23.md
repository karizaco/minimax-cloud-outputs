# Asia pre-market reactions — Monday, 23 November 2026

## TL;DR

- **Asia split three ways on the AI unwind.** Taiwan rebounded **+2.16%** to 36,811.89 on hopes of Nvidia's Jensen Huang returning plus a new short-selling ban; Korea fell **-3.31%** to 4,708.54 — its lowest close since 13 January, a fifth straight decline, with a sell-side sidecar triggered at 11:33 KST; Hong Kong was flat at **-0.12%** (22,383.74) as gold miners and physical-AI names bid against new-energy vehicles; and **Tokyo did not trade at all** (Labor Thanksgiving Day).
- **Taiwan is the market that matters at the US open, and its bounce is policy- and positioning-driven, not a demand recovery.** The Financial Supervisory Commission banned short selling below the prior close and raised the short-sale margin requirement from 130% to 150% effective Saturday, the National Stabilization Fund bought, and the TAIEX swung 1,213 points intraday from a -1.64% open to +2.16% (CNA, biggo). The published movers are all large-cap TWSE names sitting at the **+10% daily limit**, while the TPEx small-cap index fell about 3% — the rally is narrow, and it is a short-covering bounce inside a **-10.28%** five-session drawdown.
- **The US has already priced a bounce Asia never saw, so tonight's risk sits in New York, not in Asia.** S&P 500 futures are **+2.81%** and Nvidia **+8.27%** in the US pre-market, after Friday's crash — moves made *after* every Asian close, so Asia's Monday session is continuation, not signal. The numbers most likely to be mis-quoted are Taiwan's limit-locked prints, Korea's thin preferred line, and the Nikkei row, which is a **20 November** close rather than a 23 November print.

**US read-across.** Friday's US session broke the AI trade (S&P 500 -3.7%, Nvidia -14.01%, Philadelphia Semiconductor Index -10.73%), and Asia traded through that wreckage all of Monday: Korea sold another -3.31%, Taiwan bounced on a policy and a personnel headline, and Hong Kong stayed flat. Monday's US pre-market then bid the whole complex back — futures +2.81%, Nvidia +8.27% on 4.9m shares — but that bid formed *after* every Asian close, so the US open, not the Asian tape, carries the gap tonight.

- `TSM` $319.15 (Friday -9.36%), pre-market **+6.89%** on 195,721 shares — the one material pre-market print behind Asia's heaviest weight, and it is already bid.
- `UMC` **+12.21%** on 358,707 shares and `ASX` (ASE Technology) **+12.03%** on 75,974 shares — the Taiwan hardware names that limit-upped in Taipei are already up double digits in New York, so Taipei followed the US bid rather than leading it.

## Method, source health & caveats

**Method.** Eight TradingView per-country scanner pulls (4 markets × up/down), each with a `type=stock` filter, a ±2% change filter, a $2bn market-cap floor converted to local currency (JPY317bn at 158.70, TWD65.9bn at 32.97, HKD15.68bn at 7.839, KRW2.687tn at 1,343.60), a local-close floor of 5, `range=[0,20000]`, and a per-direction truncation assert (rows returned must equal `totalCount`). Four Yahoo chart fetches supplied each index's session date and levels; one `america/scan` pull (`type=dr`, 1,608 rows) supplied the US ADR read-across; the MCP-over-HTTP bridge (`market-recap`) supplied the US closes, futures, gold and yields. Market status used the date-provenance rule: index session date (Test A) is primary, the archive comparison against the 20 November artifact (Test B) is the second proof, row count (Test C) is secondary only. Japan was resolved as closed before any Japan row was composed, so the JP regional-venue resolution rule had nothing to resolve.

**Caveats.** Catalyst coverage is press-based: there is no structured Asian earnings calendar behind this routine, so a published "no company-specific catalyst identified" means no catalyst was found in the sources checked, not that none exists. The Taiwan bounce rests on a short-selling ban, a stabilization-fund report and a political headline about Nvidia's CEO — it is positioning, not earnings, and the published movers are limit prices. Korea's -3.31% came with a sidecar and a 5.11% intraday drawdown before a late institutional bid, so the close is a recovered close, not a settled one. Index percentages are taken from the Yahoo chart bars, not from the bridge's `previous_close`, which is one session stale for `^TWII` and `^HSI`. The 5-session column compares the last bar with the bar six bars back in the same fetch, so a holiday inside the window shifts the base bar by design.
