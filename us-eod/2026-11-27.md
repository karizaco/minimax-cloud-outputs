## Method & caveats

- **`change_pct` is the PRICE change** against the prior session's published close (`2026-11-25`) — the archive's convention, verifiable from the two published closes. **88 rows went ex a distribution between the sessions** and are flagged `EX_DISTRIBUTION`: on those rows the price change is a mechanical artefact, not a market move, and a screen's dividend-adjusted basis shows a different number. Worst by size: `GLU-P-A` (75.01 → 73.3), `YSAG` (41.4 → 39.8), `HII` (299.5 → 297.93), `LCII` (105.3 → 104.18), `IIIN` (26.68 → 25.67), `MDRR` (11.03 → 10.3), `NEE` (85.45 → 84.7868), `SUIS` (17.23 → 16.72), `CBT` (73.61 → 73.1175), `HFBL` (23.19 → 22.705).
- **272 rows are flagged `PRIOR_CLOSE_SUPERSEDED`: the prior session's published close for them is WRONG.** The exchange's own historical record for `2026-11-25` agrees with the vendor's price history and with the quote service's adjusted prior close, and disagrees with the close the prior session's file published - so on these rows the change % is measured on the corroborated prior close, and the prior session's file cannot reproduce it. Worst by size: `PFX` (38.38 → 40.17), `NEN` (39.8 → 41.25), `COOK` (57.37 → 58.32), `RC-P-C` (7.35 → 6.47), `BDL` (46.17 → 47.02), `VRTS` (109.8 → 109.01), `SRG-P-A` (19.44 → 20.175), `UAN` (98.98 → 99.67), `HPE-P-C` (104.5 → 105.12), `NRP` (114.6 → 113.98).
- **CSV**: `2026-11-27.csv` — **32 columns** (the base 14 session columns plus 18 derived/provenance columns). A consumer reading by position still gets the same first 14 columns. The prior session's file carried the same 32 columns, so the schema did not change.
- **Market-cap currency**: `market_cap` is **USD**. `market_cap_usd` is published explicitly and equals it. **Conversion direction: none — no FX conversion is applied to this file**, because every row's listing currency is USD. The identity to assert downstream is `cap_usd * 1.0 == cap_local`; on the international file the same identity is `usd_cap * FX_per_USD == market_cap_local` (a MULTIPLY against a local-per-USD rate).
- **Market-cap carrier rule**: the cap belongs to the ISSUER and is resolved from the vendor's own carrier row — never a volume heuristic. 4437 rows carry their own issuer's cap; 296 rows carry it from another line of the same issuer and say which one in `cap_source_symbol`. **One issuer now appears on more than one row, so a cap-weighted aggregate MUST deduplicate on `cap_source_symbol` first.**
- **The cap is published on the session's price basis.** The vendor's cap table lags the session (its rows are dated T+1), so the implied share count is taken from the vendor's carrier row, corrected by Yahoo's independent implied share count when the two readings disagree by more than 25%, and multiplied by the published close. `market_cap / close` is therefore exactly the issuer's share count on the published basis.
- **Every close carries its own settle statement** in `settle_status` (`CONFIRMED` / `UNCONFIRMED` / `NO_BAR`) and the probe round in which it last changed in `settle_round`. The session bar is taken from a **probe loop** - the close of a symbol's daily bar keeps moving for up to ~45 minutes after the close, and a bar can exist and still be provisional - so the routine pulls a stratified sample of the thinnest names plus every name that moved, round after round, and publishes when the drift goes to zero instead of sleeping a fixed time. The published close of every **CONFIRMED** row was then matched against an independent reading of the 1:00 PM ET (EARLY CLOSE - half session) auction print (Yahoo's 1-minute final bar — a different feed; where it carries the session's high or low instead of the close, as it does on thin names, the row is published as `UNCONFIRMED` rather than smoothed).
- **`data_quality`**: every row carries a flag from a closed vocabulary; a cell can hold several flags joined with `|`, in the fixed precedence NO_CAP > CAP_CARRIED > HIGH_LOW_UNAVAILABLE > RANGE_ADJUSTED > NO_HISTORY > NO_PRIOR_CLOSE > PRIOR_CLOSE_SUPERSEDED > EX_DISTRIBUTION > SPLIT_ADJUSTED > NO_PE > NO_SECTOR > OK. `OK` appears only when nothing else applies. The cell is never empty, so a consumer can always tell 'not applicable' from 'the fetch failed'.
- **Derived columns are computed once, here**, from a single price-history pull, so two routines reading this file cannot disagree about what relative volume means. `rel_volume` = session volume / mean(volume of the 30 sessions BEFORE the session); `avg_daily_dollar_volume` = mean(close x volume) over the same 30 prior sessions; `sma_20/50/200` = mean(close) over the last 20/50/200 bars ending AT the session bar; `pct_vs_sma_50/200` = (close / sma - 1) * 100; `atr_14` = mean(true range) over the last 14 bars ending at the session bar; true range = max(h-l, |h-prev_close|, |l-prev_close|); `atr_pct` = atr_14 / close * 100; `pct_from_52w_high/low` = (close - max(high) / min(low) over the last 252 bars) / that extreme * 100; `gap_pct` = (published open - prior close) / prior close * 100.
- **A close is never published outside its own range.** 459 rows take their high/low from the vendor's day range (the daily bar did not bracket the close) and 63 rows carry empty high/low because no range bracketed the close at all.
- **A missing value is empty, never zero.** A cap that cannot be resolved is empty and flagged `NO_CAP`.
- **This was a SHORT session, and `rel_volume` is depressed for EVERY name by the session length - not by liquidity.** `rel_volume` is the session's volume against the mean volume of the prior 30 FULL sessions, so a 3.5-hour session divides into a 6.5-hour denominator: the median `rel_volume` is **0.39** here against **0.84** on the prior full session (46% of it). A name reading BELOW 1.0 in `rel_volume` on this file is NOT illiquid - it traded a normal amount for the hours the market was open. The movers table's `rel_volume` column is read the same way, so a mover's relative volume has to be judged against this session's median, not against 1.0.
- **The close is the SETTLED print, never the live one.** The venue's `info` endpoint carries two blocks: `secondaryData` is stamped *"Closed at <date> 1:00 PM ET (EARLY CLOSE - half session)"* and is the official 1:00 PM ET (EARLY CLOSE - half session) auction print, while `primaryData` is the LIVE print — after the close it carries an **extended-hours** trade. A pull made after 1:00 PM ET (EARLY CLOSE - half session) that reads `primaryData` publishes an after-hours price as the session's close, and it also puts that price OUTSIDE the session's own range (measured 2026-11-12: 817 rows needed the venue's day range under the live print against 384 under the settled print). The settled block is the close of record; the live block is the tape.
- **The index and sector-ETF moves are computed from the same daily-bar series** (split-adjusted closes - the series is NOT dividend-adjusted), so they can differ from an official cash close by a few basis points. The ETF moves are a read of the tape, not the official settlement.
- **The price history is split-adjusted**, so the moving averages, the ATR and the 52-week extremes are on the same adjusted basis as the session close.
- **88 rows are flagged `EX_DISTRIBUTION` and are EXCLUDED from the movers table and from the headline**: their price change is the mechanical effect of a distribution, not a market move. Their change % is still published in the row, and the count is here so the exclusion is visible.
- **Base columns are unchanged from the published session file**: the derive stage adds columns and the carrier rule; it does not restate a single price, volume, P/E, sector or name. The only base value that changes is `market_cap` on a row that now carries its issuer's cap (the issuer-level number) instead of no cap at all.
