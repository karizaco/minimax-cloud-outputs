## Method & caveats

- **`change_pct` is the PRICE change** against the prior session's published close (`2026-11-11`) — the archive's convention, verifiable from the two published closes. **226 rows went ex a distribution between the sessions** and are flagged `EX_DISTRIBUTION`: on those rows the price change is a mechanical artefact, not a market move, and a screen's dividend-adjusted basis shows a different number. Worst by size: `SENEB` (175.4 → 148.31), `GLU-P-A` (106.9 → 103.58), `RHLD` (161.9 → 159.47), `SBAC` (190.0 → 188.78), `FANG` (181.0 → 179.8), `KEQU` (34.33 → 33.405), `AWK` (149.6 → 148.765), `PFX` (40.99 → 40.21), `MBBC` (14.89 → 14.18), `WAL` (75.84 → 75.18).
- **CSV**: `2026-11-12.csv` — **32 columns** (the base 14 session columns plus 18 derived/provenance columns). A consumer reading by position still gets the same first 14 columns. **This differs from the 30-column file published last session** (2026-11-11.csv): the added column(s) are `settle_status`, `settle_round`. A consumer that reads by POSITION is unaffected (the first 14 columns are unchanged in name and order), and a consumer that reads by NAME sees the new column(s).
- **Market-cap currency**: `market_cap` is **USD**. `market_cap_usd` is published explicitly and equals it. **Conversion direction: none — no FX conversion is applied to this file**, because every row's listing currency is USD. The identity to assert downstream is `cap_usd * 1.0 == cap_local`; on the international file the same identity is `usd_cap * FX_per_USD == market_cap_local` (a MULTIPLY against a local-per-USD rate).
- **Market-cap carrier rule**: the cap belongs to the ISSUER and is resolved from the vendor's own carrier row — never a volume heuristic. 4449 rows carry their own issuer's cap; 295 rows carry it from another line of the same issuer and say which one in `cap_source_symbol`. **One issuer now appears on more than one row, so a cap-weighted aggregate MUST deduplicate on `cap_source_symbol` first.**
- **The cap is published on the session's price basis.** The vendor's cap table lags the session (its rows are dated T+1), so the implied share count is taken from the vendor's carrier row, corrected by Yahoo's independent implied share count when the two readings disagree by more than 25%, and multiplied by the published close. `market_cap / close` is therefore exactly the issuer's share count on the published basis.
- **Every close carries its own settle statement** in `settle_status` (`CONFIRMED` / `UNCONFIRMED` / `NO_BAR`) and the probe round in which it last changed in `settle_round`. The session bar is taken from a **probe loop** - the close of a symbol's daily bar keeps moving for up to ~45 minutes after the close, and a bar can exist and still be provisional - so the routine pulls a stratified sample of the thinnest names plus every name that moved, round after round, and publishes when the drift goes to zero instead of sleeping a fixed time. The published close of every **CONFIRMED** row was then matched against an independent reading of the 16:00 ET auction print (Yahoo's 1-minute final bar — a different feed; where it carries the session's high or low instead of the close, as it does on thin names, the row is published as `UNCONFIRMED` rather than smoothed).
- **`data_quality`**: every row carries a flag from a closed vocabulary; a cell can hold several flags joined with `|`, in the fixed precedence NO_CAP > CAP_CARRIED > HIGH_LOW_UNAVAILABLE > RANGE_ADJUSTED > NO_HISTORY > NO_PRIOR_CLOSE > EX_DISTRIBUTION > SPLIT_ADJUSTED > NO_PE > NO_SECTOR > OK. `OK` appears only when nothing else applies. The cell is never empty, so a consumer can always tell 'not applicable' from 'the fetch failed'.
- **Derived columns are computed once, here**, from a single price-history pull, so two routines reading this file cannot disagree about what relative volume means. `rel_volume` = session volume / mean(volume of the 30 sessions BEFORE the session); `avg_daily_dollar_volume` = mean(close x volume) over the same 30 prior sessions; `sma_20/50/200` = mean(close) over the last 20/50/200 bars ending AT the session bar; `pct_vs_sma_50/200` = (close / sma - 1) * 100; `atr_14` = mean(true range) over the last 14 bars ending at the session bar; true range = max(h-l, |h-prev_close|, |l-prev_close|); `atr_pct` = atr_14 / close * 100; `pct_from_52w_high/low` = (close - max(high) / min(low) over the last 252 bars) / that extreme * 100; `gap_pct` = (published open - prior close) / prior close * 100.
- **A close is never published outside its own range.** 394 rows take their high/low from the vendor's day range (the daily bar did not bracket the close) and 51 rows carry empty high/low because no range bracketed the close at all.
- **A missing value is empty, never zero.** A cap that cannot be resolved is empty and flagged `NO_CAP`.
- **The close is the SETTLED print, never the live one.** The venue's `info` endpoint carries two blocks: `secondaryData` is stamped *"Closed at <date> 4:00 PM ET"* and is the official 16:00 ET auction print, while `primaryData` is the LIVE print — after the close it carries an **extended-hours** trade. A pull made after 16:00 ET that reads `primaryData` publishes an after-hours price as the session's close, and it also puts that price OUTSIDE the session's own range (measured 2026-11-12: 817 rows needed the venue's day range under the live print against 384 under the settled print). The settled block is the close of record; the live block is the tape.
- **The index and sector-ETF moves are computed from the same daily-bar series** (split-adjusted closes - the series is NOT dividend-adjusted), so they can differ from an official cash close by a few basis points. The ETF moves are a read of the tape, not the official settlement.
- **The price history is split-adjusted**, so the moving averages, the ATR and the 52-week extremes are on the same adjusted basis as the session close.
- **226 rows are flagged `EX_DISTRIBUTION` and are EXCLUDED from the movers table and from the headline**: their price change is the mechanical effect of a distribution, not a market move. Their change % is still published in the row, and the count is here so the exclusion is visible.
- **Base columns are unchanged from the published session file**: the derive stage adds columns and the carrier rule; it does not restate a single price, volume, P/E, sector or name. The only base value that changes is `market_cap` on a row that now carries its issuer's cap (the issuer-level number) instead of no cap at all.
