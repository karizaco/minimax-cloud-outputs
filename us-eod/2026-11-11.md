## Method & caveats

- **`change_pct` is the PRICE change** against the prior session's published close (`2026-11-10`) — the archive's convention, verifiable from the two published closes. **18 rows went ex a distribution between the sessions** and are flagged `EX_DISTRIBUTION`: on those rows the price change is a mechanical artefact, not a market move, and a screen's dividend-adjusted basis shows a different number. Worst by size: `KIM-P-N` (64.0 → 59.95), `MAYS` (43.6 → 42.15), `TRTX-P-C` (16.7 → 16.4201), `BUDA` (7.31 → 7.075), `BC-P-C` (21.75 → 21.6101), `DTSQ` (11.7 → 11.5999), `SVIV` (10.16 → 10.06), `SHPH` (5.34 → 5.26), `FUND` (9.92 → 9.845), `CVR` (9.3 → 9.25).
- **CSV**: `2026-11-11.csv` — **30 columns** (the base 14 session columns plus 16 derived/provenance columns). A consumer reading by position still gets the same first 14 columns.
- **Market-cap currency**: `market_cap` is **USD**. `market_cap_usd` is published explicitly and equals it. **Conversion direction: none — no FX conversion is applied to this file**, because every row's listing currency is USD. The identity to assert downstream is `cap_usd * 1.0 == cap_local`; on the international file the same identity is `usd_cap * FX_per_USD == market_cap_local` (a MULTIPLY against a local-per-USD rate).
- **Market-cap carrier rule**: the cap belongs to the ISSUER and is resolved from the vendor's own carrier row — never a volume heuristic. 4441 rows carry their own issuer's cap; 294 rows carry it from another line of the same issuer and say which one in `cap_source_symbol`. **One issuer now appears on more than one row, so a cap-weighted aggregate MUST deduplicate on `cap_source_symbol` first.**
- **`data_quality`**: every row carries a flag from a closed vocabulary; a cell can hold several flags joined with `|`, in the fixed precedence NO_CAP > CAP_CARRIED > HIGH_LOW_UNAVAILABLE > RANGE_ADJUSTED > NO_HISTORY > NO_PRIOR_CLOSE > EX_DISTRIBUTION > SPLIT_ADJUSTED > NO_PE > NO_SECTOR > OK. `OK` appears only when nothing else applies. The cell is never empty, so a consumer can always tell 'not applicable' from 'the fetch failed'.
- **Derived columns are computed once, here**, from a single price-history pull, so two routines reading this file cannot disagree about what relative volume means. `rel_volume` = session volume / mean(volume of the 30 sessions BEFORE the session); `avg_daily_dollar_volume` = mean(close x volume) over the same 30 prior sessions; `sma_20/50/200` = mean(close) over the last 20/50/200 bars ending AT the session bar; `pct_vs_sma_50/200` = (close / sma - 1) * 100; `atr_14` = mean(true range) over the last 14 bars ending at the session bar; true range = max(h-l, |h-prev_close|, |l-prev_close|); `atr_pct` = atr_14 / close * 100; `pct_from_52w_high/low` = (close - max(high) / min(low) over the last 252 bars) / that extreme * 100; `gap_pct` = (published open - prior close) / prior close * 100.
- **A close is never published outside its own range.** 4828 rows publish a range and 63 publish none; of the rows with a close, **18** carry NO range at all, because neither the daily bar nor the venue's day range brackets their close (thin names whose vendor bar prints a close outside its own OHLC range) — the range is left empty rather than invented. The ranges that are published come from the venue's own day range (4751 rows), the quote service's day range (57 rows) and the daily bar (20 rows); 410 rows are flagged `RANGE_ADJUSTED` because the daily bar did not bracket the published close.
- **A missing value is empty, never zero.** A cap that cannot be resolved is empty and flagged `NO_CAP`.
- **The index and sector-ETF moves are computed from the same daily-bar series** (split-adjusted closes - the series is NOT dividend-adjusted), so they can differ from an official cash close by a few basis points. The ETF moves are a read of the tape, not the official settlement.
- **The price history is split-adjusted**, so the moving averages, the ATR and the 52-week extremes are on the same adjusted basis as the session close.
- **18 rows are flagged `EX_DISTRIBUTION` and are EXCLUDED from the movers table and from the headline**: their price change is the mechanical effect of a distribution, not a market move. Their change % is still published in the row, and the count is here so the exclusion is visible.
- **Base columns are unchanged from the published session file**: the derive stage adds columns and the carrier rule; it does not restate a single price, volume, P/E, sector or name. The only base value that changes is `market_cap` on a row that now carries its issuer's cap (the issuer-level number) instead of no cap at all.

- **The close of record is the venue's settled print** — Nasdaq's own `secondaryData` (`Closed at 4:00 PM ET`), not the vendor's daily-bar close. The two are within 0.01% on 2652 of 4774 rows, within 0.1% on 3847 and within 0.5% on 4460; on **2302** rows the daily bar still holds a pre-auction print, and on **314** of those the gap is 0.5% or more (**14** rows above 5%). CNBC/CTA's last trade, when it is stamped 16:00:00-05:00, agrees with the venue's print on every sampled name — including the largest gaps (MDBH 1.51 vs the bar's 1.7195, TVGN 5.47 vs 6.00, VRM 6.45 vs 6.99). The archive's own 2026-11-10 file is on the venue's basis (HAIN's 11-10 close 0.135 = Nasdaq's consolidated record, against Yahoo's 0.14), so this run keeps the archive's basis and the `change_pct` anchor stays reconcilable across the two files.
- **117 rows carry no session bar** in Yahoo's daily-bar feed, so they have no derived columns and are flagged `NO_HISTORY`. **72** of them still publish the venue's settled close (the bar feed is simply silent on those names); CNBC/CTA shows a 16:00 ET print on **70** of the 117. **45** rows publish no close at all, because no source carries one — a row with no print keeps empty price cells rather than a borrowed price.
- **Market-cap basis**: the vendor's `valuation` cap rows are dated **2026-11-10** (the prior session) and sit on that session's close basis, so they are **rescaled onto this session's close** (4081 rows) before publication; 62 rows could not be rescaled (their prior bar is not the prior session) and keep the vendor's figure. 318 rows have no vendor cap and take the quote service's market cap instead; 1 cap(s) whose implied share count is implausible are published EMPTY and flagged `NO_CAP`.
- **Universe delta vs the 2026-11-10 file**: 4891 rows pulled against 4893 published rows last session (1 newly tagged, 3 dropped by the vendor) — the vendor's `country_iso` tag is nullable, so the delta is published.
- **`change_pct` is the PRICE change** against the prior session's published close (`2026-11-10`): 4814 rows had no change and were filled from that anchor, 0 were corrected, 32 could not be verified. 18 rows are flagged `EX_DISTRIBUTION` and 6 `SPLIT_ADJUSTED`.
- **The price history is the vendor's daily bar, in TWO passes** (16:05 ET and 16:35 ET); the later pass wins the session bar, and it changed the session close on **1957 of 4907** names — the bar keeps drifting for ~40 minutes after the close. Its session bar's close is replaced by the venue's settled print before the derived columns are computed, so one row carries one price.
- **The Yahoo 1-minute final bar** (`O=H=L=C`, volume 0) is the auction-print cross-check: it agrees with the published close on 3847 of 4762 names and disagrees by more than 0.1% on 915. It is NOT the price source — on thin names Yahoo's synthetic bar carries the session's high or low rather than the close, which is why the venue's print is published instead.
