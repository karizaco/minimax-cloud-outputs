The caveat that matters is provenance. The close of record is the exchange's own settlement stamp — every close in this file is stamped *"Closed at Nov 3, 2026 4:00 PM ET"*, not an intraday print — and it was cross-checked against an independent exchange quote service on the session's largest movers (12 of 12 sampled closes matched exactly). The daily-bar feed was used for the session range and the opening print, and it lags per symbol; where its range could not bracket the settled close, the vendor's own day range was used instead and the range was left empty only when neither source could bracket it.

Two gaps are real and are left empty rather than filled: 5 symbols have no session volume (dormant preferred classes and names that did not trade), and the vendor's reference table carries no GICS sector for 406 of them.

## Notes

- Every numeric field is published to 4 significant figures with trailing zeros stripped; empty cells mean the datum was not available and are never written as `N/A`.
- `close` is the exchange's settlement-stamped close. The daily-bar feed's close was not used where it disagreed: the settlement stamp won in every case, and the two feeds agreed within 0.05% on all 4826 symbols carrying both.
- `volume` is the settled session volume from the quote feed, which agrees exactly with the independent exchange quote service on the sampled movers.
- The vendor's valuation table still carries its 2026-11-02 session rows (its EOD ingest lags), so each company cap is rescaled by today's close-to-prior-close ratio; a cap is written on the vendor's **carrier row only**, and sibling class rows carry none, so each company contributes exactly once to a cap-weighted aggregate.
- Preferred lines carry the issuer's common-share count in every quote service, so their cap would be the company's rather than the line's; no cap is written on preferred rows.
- The session range always brackets the settled close. Where the daily bar could not bracket it, the vendor's own day range was used; 35 rows had no bracketing range from either source and are written with empty `high`/`low`.
- Market cap is the vendor's company-level figure; do not build cap-weighted sector aggregates from this file without re-checking them against the sector ETFs.
