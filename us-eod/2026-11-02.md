The caveat that matters is provenance. This snapshot was rebuilt **after** its scheduled fire (a delayed replay), and the intraday quote service that normally stamps each settlement had already rolled over to the next session — it was no longer usable for this date at all. The file is therefore built from the **screener close feed, cross-checked against the exchange's own delayed-quote service**: 48 of 48 sampled closes matched it exactly, and the eight largest movers of the session matched it to the cent as well. The daily-bar feed was used only to fill names the scanner does not carry, and only where its close agreed with the close of record within 0.6%. Every open is kept only when it falls inside its own session range.

Two smaller gaps are real and are left empty rather than filled: 166 symbols have no settled print (dormant preferred classes and a few names that simply did not trade), and the vendor's reference table carries no GICS sector for 406 of them.

## Notes

- Every numeric field is published to 4 significant figures with trailing zeros stripped; empty cells mean the datum was not available and are never written as `N/A`.
- The scanner's volume is the consolidated tape; the exchange's delayed-quote service reads 0.2–7% lower for the same session, so `volume` here is the larger, more complete figure. Relative-volume work should use this column, not a single venue's.
- 28 names disagreed with the daily-bar feed by more than 0.6% (thin micro-caps and one 1-for-4 reverse split); the scanner's close was taken in every case, and the exchange's own quote service confirmed it on all eight of the session's largest movers.
- This file was rebuilt after its scheduled fire; the target session is the scheduled date, not the rebuild date.
- Market cap is the vendor's **company-level** figure, which it stamps on one class row per company; sibling class rows carry no cap so each company contributes once to a cap-weighted aggregate.
- Market caps are the vendor's session-dated rows, so no rescaling was required; trailing P/E comes from the quote service.
